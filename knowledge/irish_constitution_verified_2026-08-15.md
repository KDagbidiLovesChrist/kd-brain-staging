## Corrections to what Claude had told King

- INCOMPLETE, and it changes the meaning. Claude told King the Fifth Amendment deleted Article 44.1.2. It deleted TWO subsections, 44.1.2 (the Catholic special position) and 44.1.3 (which named the Church of Ireland, the Presbyterian Church in Ireland, the Methodist Church in Ireland, the Religious Society of Friends in Ireland and the Jewish Congregations). The Constitution's own amending Acts table describes it in exactly those terms. The amendment did not demote Catholicism into a named list, it abolished the list, so the Constitution now names no church at all. That is why an Orthodox Christian is not a second-tier denomination under the text today.
- DATE. Claude said the deletion happened 'in 1972 by referendum'. The referendum was 7 December 1972, but the words stayed in the Constitution until the President signed the Act on 5 January 1973. Three independent official records agree: the Act's own long title bracket [5th January, 1973], the DATE OF SIGNATURE column in the Constitution's amending Acts table, and the Oireachtas API field dateSigned 1973-01-05. Cite it as the 'Fifth Amendment of the Constitution Act, 1972' per its own section 2(2), and expect to see the Oireachtas style it 'Act, 1973 (C5 of 1973)' for the same instrument.
- MISLEADING BY OMISSION, and this is the most important one for a man grounding a belief system in this text. Claude summarised Article 44 today as freedom of conscience, no endowment, no discrimination. All three are there, but Article 44.1 still stands and still reads: 'The State acknowledges that the homage of public worship is due to Almighty God. It shall hold His Name in reverence, and shall respect and honour religion.' Article 44 is not a secular provision. I confirmed that no amendment other than the Fifth has ever touched Article 44, by fetching all 32 enacted amendment Acts and searching each one.
- NUMBERING. 'Article 44.1.2' has not existed since 5 January 1973. The Article now runs 44.1, then 44.2.1 to 44.2.6, with those final numerals printed as degree signs. Always date the old citation: 'Article 44.1.2 as enacted in 1937, deleted 1973'.
- CORRECTION TO THE PREVIOUS FACT CHECK, not to Claude. The previous check told King 'Today's Article 44.2 is The State guarantees not to endow any religion'. That is wrong. Article 44.2 is the whole section holding six subsections. The endowment clause is Article 44.2.2, and the Constitution's own index confirms it with the entry 'endowment 44.2.2'. The previous check committed the exact miscitation it was warning against.
- CORRECTION TO THE PREVIOUS FACT CHECK, and this is the sharpest one. It told King that before 2018 Article 40.6.1.i read 'seditious, indecent or blasphemous matter'. It did not. The Thirty-seventh Amendment Act 2018 section 1(b) substituted 'seditious' for 'blasphemous, seditious,', so the original read 'blasphemous, seditious, or indecent matter', with blasphemous FIRST. The Irish confirms the same order, 'diamhaslach no ceannairceach' became 'ceannairceach'. A reordered quotation presented as original wording is precisely the error type that survives a casual look.
- SCOPE. Article 44.2.1 guarantees freedom of conscience and free profession and practice of religion 'to every citizen', not to every person. King is an Irish citizen so it reaches him, but he should not repeat it as universal on the face of the text.
- FOUR SUBSECTIONS were left out of the Article 44 picture, not three. 44.2.4 (State aid to schools must not discriminate between denominations, and no child may be prejudiced for attending a publicly funded school without taking religious instruction), 44.2.5 (every denomination may manage its own affairs, own and administer property, and maintain institutions), 44.2.6 (denominational property may not be diverted except for necessary public works, with compensation), and, missed by the previous check as well, Article 42.3.1 ('The State shall not oblige parents in violation of their conscience and lawful preference to send their children to schools established by the State, or to any particular type of school designated by the State'). For a catechumen in a minority church these are the practical protections.
- DATE, on blasphemy. Claude said the statutory offence was repealed in 2019. The Act was signed 21 December 2019 but section 5(2) left commencement to the Minister, and S.I. No. 13/2020, sealed by Charles Flanagan on 16 January 2020, appointed 17 January 2020. Correct form: passed 2019, in force 17 January 2020. There were 27 days when a repealing Act sat on the books beside a live offence.
- NOT A CORRECTION, A CONFIRMATION TO LOCK IN. Claude wrote 'Holy Catholic Apostolic and Roman Church' and that is exactly right. There is no 'and' after 'Catholic'. Two official sources quote it identically and I opened both. Many reputable-looking sources garble it. Quote it as Claude gave it.
- NEW, AND IT FAVOURS KING. The Irish text of the Preamble is not a straight translation. Where the English says 'our Divine Lord, Jesus Christ', the Irish says 'Iosa Criost, ar dTiarna Dia', Jesus Christ our Lord God. Under Article 25.5.4 the Irish text prevails in case of conflict. If King is building anything on the Preamble, the Irish is the operative text and it is the stronger one. Have a fluent Irish speaker or the solicitor confirm before he relies on it.
- NEW CAVEAT ON 'THE PREAMBLE IS UNAMENDED'. True for the English, and I proved it Act by Act. But the published Constitution's own header states that the Irish text 'has been altered so as to make it conform to modern standardised Irish'. That is an orthographic tidy-up by the Taoiseach under Article 25.5.1, not an amendment under Article 46, but it means the printed Irish Preamble is not letter for letter the 1937 enrolled Irish. Worth knowing given that the Irish prevails.

The Law Reform Commission's consolidated Revised Acts annotates Defamation Act 2009 ss.36 and 37 as "Repealed (17.01.2020)". - Claim 4 is true but materially incomplete. Article 40.6.1.i carries three qualifications, not one: the chapeau "subject to public order and morality", the education of public opinion paragraph (organs of public opinion "shall not be used to undermine public order or morality or the authority of the State"), and the standing provision that publication or utterance of seditious or indecent matter is an offence.

The sentence "The publication or utterance of seditious or indecent matter is an offence which shall be punishable in accordance with law" is still in Article 40.6.1.i today. Only "blasphemous" and its trailing comma came out.
- NEW, and important for King's argument. The constitutional words about sedition survive, but the common law offence of seditious libel does not. Defamation Act 2009 section 35 reads: "The common law offences of defamatory libel, seditious libel and obscene libel are abolished." It is in force with no amendment annotation in the Law Reform Commission revised text. So sedition was cleared out of the common law in 2009, eleven years before blasphemy. Do not say the 2019 Act "said nothing about sedition and therefore sedition survives", that is wrong.

The deleted 44.1.3° named the Church of Ireland, the Presbyterian Church in Ireland, the Methodist Church in Ireland, the Religious Society of Friends in Ireland and the Jewish Congregations. The Fifth Amendment removed the whole scheme of naming denominations, not just the Catholic clause. If King tells the story as "Ireland removed the Catholic Church's special position", he is telling half of it. The official 1972 ballot paper statement is titled "RECOGNITION OF SPECIFIED RELIGIONS".

The most recent Irish non-compete case in which the constitutional argument was raised ended with the employee restrained.

It is the originating 1972 High Court authority in which the right was first recognised. The leading modern authority is NVH v Minister for Justice [2017] IESC 35, a seven judge Supreme Court, and it QUESTIONS the doctrine rather than reinforcing it.

Section 4 repealed the STATUTORY provisions, section 36 of the Defamation Act 2009 (publication or utterance of blasphemous matter) and section 37 (seizure of copies of blasphemous statements). For an argument about freedom of belief, convictions and certitudes are not the same word.
