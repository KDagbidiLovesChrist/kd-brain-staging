Here are the actionable video-editing techniques from the tutorial, tailored for a cinematic product/service showcase ad:

1.  **TITLE / TOPIC**
    Four Advanced Editing Techniques to Go Viral

2.  **TOP TECHNIQUES TAUGHT**

    1.  **Animated Stock Characters**
        *   **How to do it**:
            1.  Find stock photos of people in relevant poses.
            2.  Remove background using Quick Selection Tool > Select Subject > Invert Selection > Delete in Photoshop/Photopea.
            3.  Add a logo or a cut-out head/neck onto the stock body.
            4.  In your video editor (60fps):
                *   **Entry Animation**: Move character off-screen, create a Position keyframe. Fifteen frames later, create a second Position keyframe with the character fully on-screen. Ease the keyframes for smooth motion. Save as a reusable preset (e.g., "Character In").
                *   **Subtle Movement**: Apply a "handheld camera shake" preset to the character clip (search online for free presets for your editor).
                *   **Changing Poses**: For different actions, edit multiple stock character images side-by-side in the timeline, each with the "Character In" and "camera shake" presets.
        *   **When to use it**: Replace direct speaking, act out scenarios, illustrate flashbacks, inject humor, or visually tell a story to boost engagement.

    2.  **Counter Effect**
        *   **How to do it**:
            1.  Create a new sequence in your video editor.
            2.  Add a "Timecode" effect to a transparent video layer.
            3.  Configure Timecode settings: Remove field symbol, set format to "Frames," and source to "Clip Timecode" (to ensure it starts counting from 0).
            4.  Stretch the transparent video clip to display the desired maximum number.
            5.  Center and scale the timecode on the screen.
            6.  For specific use cases (e.g., displaying growth): Overlay this counter sequence (imported as a single clip) over relevant visuals, cut it to start at a desired initial number, and then speed up the clip to rapidly count upwards.
        *   **When to use it**: Visually represent large, growing numbers like money earned, views, subscribers, or any quantitative progress.

    3.  **Graphs**
        *   **How to do it**:
            1.  Draw a graph using your editor's pen or shape tool (e.g., Premiere Pro Pen Tool) or in a photo/motion graphics editor.
            2.  Remove any fill, set the stroke to "Center," and adjust its thickness.
            3.  Animate its reveal: Apply a "Linear Wipe" effect.
            4.  Set the "Wipe Angle" to approximately the opposite direction the graph line goes.
            5.  Keyframe "Transition Completion" from 100% (fully hidden) to 0% (fully revealed) over time.
        *   **When to use it**: Illustrate trends, show growth or decline, or visualize data in an engaging way.

    4.  **Subtitles**
        *   **How to do it**:
            1.  Generate auto-subtitles using your editor's built-in feature (e.g., Premiere Pro: Window > Text > Create captions from transcript).
            2.  Adjust settings to display only 1-2 words per caption for readability.
            3.  **Styling**:
                *   Keep subtitles relatively small and positioned at the bottom of the screen.
                *   Add a black stroke or subtle drop shadow/glow for contrast against varied backgrounds.
                *   Choose an easily readable font (e.g., SF Pro Bold).
                *   For animation, use simple cuts synchronized with speech; avoid overly dramatic pop-ins, distracting colors, or outlines.
        *   **When to use it**: Boost viewer retention, clarify spoken content, and ensure accessibility for viewers watching without sound.

3.  **THE 3 MOST IMPORTANT TAKEAWAYS**

    1.  **Dynamic Visual Engagement**: Never let the screen be static. Use animated elements, characters, graphs, and counters to keep viewers visually stimulated and prevent boredom.
    2.  **Information Density via Motion Graphics**: Convey complex or numerical information quickly and engagingly using animated graphs and counters, rather than just spoken words or static text.
    3.  **Subtle Polish for Professionalism**: Prioritize smooth animations (60fps, eased keyframes) and restrained, complementary graphics (especially subtitles) over flashy or overly dramatic effects, ensuring the content remains the star.
