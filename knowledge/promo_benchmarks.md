## PART 1 · THE 5 REFERENCE EXAMPLES

### Example 1 · Alex Hormozi (Acquisition.com / $100M Offers)
**Platform:** YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels / TikTok
**Channel:** @AlexHormozi | 2.84M YT subs, 3M IG followers
**Representative video style:** any 60, 90s "how I made $X" or "why agencies fail" Shorts
**Performance evidence:** Consistent 500K, 5M+ views per Short; widely studied as the defining
style benchmark for B2B/service content.
**URL reference:** https://www.youtube.com/@AlexHormozi (search "Shorts" tab)

**What makes it work (craft notes):**
- Hook (0, 3s): Talking head opens with a direct, contrarian or result-led statement.
  No intro music sting, no logo, no title card. The words ARE the hook.
  Example: "Most agency owners are broke because they charge for time, not outcomes."
- Captions: ALL-CAPS, bold sans-serif (Montserrat Black / TheBoldFont), 1, 3 words
  per card, word-by-word reveal synced to speech. The active word highlighted in
  yellow/brand colour; all others white. Thick black stroke, essential, not optional.
  Text occupies roughly the lower third at ~13, 15% of frame height.
- Pacing: Jump cuts every 1, 3 seconds. All pauses, filler words, and dead air removed.
  Average shot length under 2 seconds. Creates density, viewer feels they cannot
  look away.
- Proof/product: No screen recordings. Social proof is verbal ("we closed $2.3M
  last month") + B-roll of whiteboards or stat overlays. Result stated in concrete
  numbers, not adjectives.
- Music/sound: None (silent floor) or very low ambient. Voice is the full soundscape.
  This is intentional, the voice quality carries all emotional weight.
- CTA: Single soft CTA in last 3, 5 seconds: "Comment 'SCALE' and I'll send you
  the framework." Comment-bait triggers algorithm. No hard-sell URL read.
- **ONE THING IT DOES BEST:** The jump-cut density creates urgency. The viewer
  physically cannot leave because there are no gaps to decide to scroll.

### Example 2 · Chris Donnelly (@donnellycss)
**Platform:** TikTok / LinkedIn
**Followers:** 772K TikTok, 900K monthly LinkedIn views
**Niche:** Agency scaling, SMMA, founder operations
**Performance evidence:** 62K likes on individual videos; 2.9K average engagements;
0.39% engagement rate at scale (strong for B2B). Source: Favikon profile analysis.
**URL:** https://www.tiktok.com/@donnellycss

**What makes it work (craft notes):**
- Hook (0, 3s): Opens with a contrarian problem statement. No face-hide.
  Talking head, direct eye contact, minimal background. The problem IS the hook.
  Example opener: "Marketing agencies are finished… here's how I'd build one
  in 2024 from scratch."
- Captions: Uses on-screen text overlays but deliberately sparse, key phrases
  only, not full transcripts. Lets the voice lead. This is the opposite of Hormozi's
  word-by-word style and works for a more senior B2B audience.
- Pacing: Slower than Hormozi. 3, 5 second shots. "Micro-lesson" structure:
  one problem, one reframe, one action. Feels like advice from a peer, not a sales
  pitch.
- Proof: Verbal, revenue figures, client outcomes, operational specifics.
  No screen recordings. No before/after reveals.
- Music: None. Silence signals authority in the B2B space.
- CTA: Conversational close, "What's stopping you from doing this?" Engagement
  question that drives comments over clicks.
- **ONE THING IT DOES BEST:** The "problem → reframe → action" 3-beat structure
  makes every 60s feel complete and useful, not promotional. The viewer gets value
  before they're asked for anything.

### Example 3 · Nick Saraev (@nick_saraev)
**Platform:** Instagram Reels, YouTube
**Followers:** 360K Instagram (verified), 400K YouTube
**Niche:** AI automation, n8n, agency systems, Make.com workflows
**Performance evidence:** Reels regularly referenced and reshared in AI community;
"Comment AUTOMATION to get the workflow" CTA mechanic drives high comment velocity.
His Claude Code tutorial crossed 1.5M YT views + 20M+ cross-platform views.
**URL:** https://www.instagram.com/nick_saraev/
**Example reel:** https://www.instagram.com/reel/DITJZBdscgo/ (April 11, 2025)

**What makes it work (craft notes):**
- Hook (0, 3s): Screen recording of a live n8n or Make workflow running, with a
  result-first text overlay. Example: "This workflow saved my client 40 hours/week."
  The product IS visible immediately, no warm-up.
- Captions: Clean white bold captions (not word-by-word Hormozi style). More like
  subtitle chunks, 4, 6 words at a time. Readable, not flashy.
- Pacing: Medium tempo, 2, 4 second shots. Screen recordings with zooms and
  cursor highlights to guide attention. B-roll of results/dashboards interspersed.
- Proof/product: Direct: live screen recordings of automation workflows, client
  dashboards, revenue screenshots. This is the core proof format for the niche.
  "Show the machine running" is more convincing than any testimonial.
- Music: Light lo-fi or ambient. Not beat-synced. Background only.
- CTA: Comment-gating ("comment 'SYSTEM' for the template"), this is the
  single most effective CTA mechanic for this niche. Low friction, high algorithm
  reward, builds list via DM automation.
- **ONE THING IT DOES BEST:** The comment-gate CTA ("Comment X to get Y") is
  perfectly matched to the niche. Every viewer who engages signals genuine intent,
  and the DM automation closes the loop. It turns a promo into a lead-gen system.

### Example 4 · Jordan Welch (D2C / Brand-Building Vertical)
**Platform:** YouTube, TikTok
**Subscribers:** 1.5M YouTube
**Niche:** Brand building, product launches, ecommerce + service hybrid
**Performance evidence:** 1.5M YT subs; multiple Shorts with 1M+ views. Widely cited
in the short-form creator economy as a case study for B2B-adjacent service content.
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/@JordanWelch

**What makes it work (craft notes):**
- Hook (0, 3s): Visual reveal first, shows a result, a screen, a number, THEN
  the voice follows. The visual does the stopping; the voice does the explaining.
  "I built a $50K/month business in 90 days using this one page." shown on screen
  as the hook, not just spoken.
- Captions: Hormozi-influenced, bold, word-by-word, but with more cinematic B-roll
  underneath. Not just a talking head. The captions float over relevant footage.
- Pacing: Fast (1, 2s cuts) but uses scene variety: talking head → screen → result
  screenshot → talking head. This multi-layer pacing prevents fatigue.
- Proof: Numbers + screen recordings + "reveal" moments. The reveal structure
  (building anticipation then showing the result) is borrowed from product-launch
  content and works extremely well for service businesses.
- Music: Upbeat, low-volume background track. Beat-synced transitions on key cuts
  (not every cut, just the major scene changes). This is the "punctuation" function
  of music.
- CTA: "Link in bio" + "subscribe for the full breakdown." Two-step CTA:
  immediate action + retention ask.
- **ONE THING IT DOES BEST:** Result-first visual hook. The number or outcome is
  on screen in the first 2 seconds. The viewer's brain anchors on the result before
  they decide whether to keep watching. This is the highest-leverage single change
  any service promo can make.

## PART 2 · WHAT THE BEST ONES DO (BUILDABLE CHECKLIST)

These 10 rules are drawn from all 5 examples plus the Joyspace 2026 analysis,
Sovran Hook/Body/CTA framework, and Vidico promo research.

**Rule 1, Result-first visual hook (0, 2s)**
The outcome, number, or "wow" moment must be VISIBLE on screen in the first
2 seconds, not just spoken. A text overlay with a concrete number beats
any intro animation.

**Rule 2, 3-second hard rule: no logo, no music sting, no title card**
Every frame from 0, 3s must serve the hook. Intros, logo reveals, and brand
idents belong on broadcast TV, not short-form service content. Cut them.

**Rule 3, Caption font: heavy, contrasted, load-bearing**
All-caps, bold condensed sans (Montserrat Black, Anton, or TheBoldFont).
Thick black stroke, without it, captions vanish on bright footage.
1, 3 words per card, word-by-word sync. The active word: brand highlight colour.
Minimum 13% of frame height. Thin fonts = amateur signal.

**Rule 4, Jump cuts remove ALL dead air**
Every pause, filler, breath that isn't load-bearing gets cut. Average shot
length under 2 seconds for the body section. Density = retention.

**Rule 5, Show the machine or the result, not the concept**
Screen recordings of live dashboards, workflows running, or client results on
screen beats any spoken claim. For AI/web services: show the chatbot answering,
the site loading, the dashboard updating. Proof is visual, not verbal.

**Rule 6, Comment-gate CTA for this niche**
"Comment 'X' and I'll send you Y" is the highest-ROI CTA for AI/service content.
It triggers algorithmic amplification (comments = engagement signal),
builds a lead list via DM automation, and filters for genuine intent.
Replace soft "link in bio" CTAs with this.

**Rule 7, Music is punctuation, not wallpaper**
Low-mix ambient or no music for B2B authority positioning (Donnelly, Hormozi).
If using music: beat-sync only the major scene transitions, not every cut.
Never let music compete with VO volume. VO is the master channel.

**Rule 8, Single proof point per video, stated in numbers**
"Saved 40 hours/week" beats "dramatically increases efficiency."
"£2,300 site in 48 hours" beats "premium results."
One number, stated twice (once spoken, once as caption overlay), is more
persuasive than five vague claims.

**Rule 9, 4-beat narrative arc even in 60 seconds**
Origin → Obstacle → Method → Result. This structure creates emotional investment.
Viewers don't share ads, they share stories. Even 60-second promos can have
a narrative arc that makes them feel like a mini-documentary.

**Rule 10, Dynamic Minimalism over Hormozi-default (2026 standard)**
The aggressive yellow-green word-pop is now saturated. The 2026 professional
signal is: clean sans-serif, white text with subtle shadow, brand-colour keyword
highlights, smooth fades not jarring pops. Same cognitive mechanics, better brand positioning. Especially important for B2B/premium clients.

## PART 3 · KING'S CUT vs THE BENCHMARK

**King's current cut spec (60s, black+gold, faceless):**
Bold Anton captions + gold accent words | Real full-screen footage of live builds
(website, chatbot, SaaS dashboard) | George VO deep-diving each | Free-sample CTA
| Black+gold palette throughout

### WHERE IT ALREADY MATCHES THE BENCHMARK

- **Caption font (Anton, bold, accented)**, Anton is a legitimate benchmark font.
  Gold accent words match the "brand-colour keyword highlight" standard.
  This is at professional level.
- **Real footage of live builds**, Showing the actual product running (chatbot
  answering, site loading) is exactly what Nick Saraev does and is the highest-
  credibility proof format in this niche. This is King's biggest asset.
- **Faceless format**, Correct for the niche. Not a liability.
- **60-second length**, Right in the optimal zone for service content (30, 90s).
- **Deep-dive VO (George voice)**, A distinctive, warm, professional VO voice
  is a strong differentiator. Most competitors use generic TTS.

### WHERE IT IS BEHIND THE BENCHMARK

- **Hook (0, 2s):** Unknown from description, but most builds open on a brand
  element or intro beat, not an instant result-first visual. If the first frame
  is not a concrete result (a number, a before/after, or the live product in action),
  the video is losing 30, 50% of potential viewers before they're hooked.
  **Fix:** Frame 1 = the most impressive live build with a number overlay.
  E.g.: "Built in 48 hours. Live chatbot. Real client.", on screen, not spoken.

- **No comment-gate CTA:** "Free sample" is a low-friction offer but it requires
  the viewer to click away. Comment-gating ("Comment 'SITE' and I'll send you
  a free mockup of your business") converts better on Instagram/TikTok, triggers
  the algorithm, and collects warm leads in DMs. This is the single biggest
  missing mechanic.

- **Pacing / jump-cut density:** George VO deep-dives suggest slower, more
  explanatory pacing. This is appropriate for YouTube long-form but loses short-form
  viewers fast. The "deep dive" energy should be compressed into fast jump-cut
  clips, the same information, delivered at 2x visual speed, with the VO as
  the connective tissue.

- **Single proof point per video:** A 60-second cut covering multiple services
  (website + chatbot + dashboard) dilutes each proof. The benchmark formula is
  one service, one number, one story per video. A series of 5 x 30-second
  single-service promos will outperform one 60-second multi-service reel.

- **Music:** No information on current music usage, but if it is beat-synced
  to every cut rather than only key transitions, it will read as template content.
  Music should serve the VO, never compete with it.
