# Logos · The Discovery Call Deck (generic, reusable)

**What this is:** the deck King runs on every booked discovery call, whoever the prospect is.
Swap nothing except the prospect's business type on Slide 2 and the worked example on Slide 10.

**Length:** 14 slides plus an appendix. Roughly 20 minutes of talking, leaving 25 minutes for the
actual conversation, which is the point of the call.

**Hard rules baked in:** nothing on any slide is a promise. Every number is either a real market
figure, King's own real price, or clearly labelled as an estimate. Tier 1 and Tier 2 are visually
separated so nobody can mistake the roadmap for something they can buy today.

**Brand:** Navy #0A1628 backgrounds, Off-White #F8F6F1 body text, Gold #C9A84C on key numbers only,
Forest Green #2D6A4F reserved for "available now" and the closing CTA. Roadmap items in muted grey,
never gold, never green.

## SLIDE 6 · What it does, end to end

**On screen:**

> **Seven pieces, then three things about how it runs. The whole shape of a build.**
>
> **1. Intake.** Your business gets recorded once, properly. Everything after this reads from it.
> **2. Diagnosis.** A conversation, not a form. The questions adapt to your answers.
> **3. The Clarity Brief.** One page: the real bottleneck, the one system that fixes it, an honest
> estimate of what it's worth. You approve it or you don't.
> **4. What gets built.** A landing page written to your diagnosis, campaign graphics and captions
> that match your brand, and a follow-up sequence over WhatsApp and email.
> **5. Lead pipeline.** Every enquiry captured and visible. Nothing sitting in a DM you forgot.
> **6. Booking and payment.** They book themselves. You stop playing calendar tennis.
> **7. Your dashboard.** One screen that shows it working. No jargon on it anywhere.
>
> **Three things a chatbot can't do.**
>
> **It checks its own work before you see it.** Rather than one expensive AI doing everything,
> Logos splits the job between a small team of cheaper, specialised helpers, with a more careful
> one checking anything that matters before it's sent or trusted. Measure twice, cut once, the way
> a good tradesperson checks a measurement before cutting. The work stays right, the running cost
> stays low, and that saving goes into your price rather than my margin.
>
> **It gets sharper about your business the longer you use it.** Everything it knows is marked
> either "you told me this" or "we measured this." Day one runs mostly on the first. Week by week
> those starting figures get replaced by your own real numbers, so what it recommends gets more
> accurate rather than staler. Off-the-shelf software is the same on day 300 as it was on day 1.
>
> **It works while you sleep.** This isn't a chat window you have to remember to open. It runs to
> a schedule, on a machine in the cloud rather than on your laptop, so it keeps going when your
> laptop is shut. A follow-up gets sent, a report gets drafted, something gets checked. You read
> what happened next time you look.
>
> **Nothing publishes until you say so.** Not overnight either. Nothing reaches a customer without
> your approval, and that's built in rather than a setting you have to find.

**King says:**
"I've built this exact flow as a working system already, for a founder-led business here in the
UK. I can't name them, they haven't given me permission, but I can put it on screen and click
through it live if you want to see it rather than take my word for it."

**Presenter note:** Have the working demo open in a second tab before the call starts. Offer it

## SLIDE 9 · How I price, before I show you a number

**On screen:**

> **I don't price by hours. I price against what the problem is costing you.**
>
> The method, plainly:
>
> **1.** We work out what the problem costs you over a year. Hours lost, or leads lost, times what
> they're worth.
> **2.** The project fee lands at roughly **10% to 20% of that yearly figure.**
> **3.** I show you the maths. You check it against what you know about your own business.
>
> **Why that's fair rather than arbitrary:** if a build is only worth a small amount to you, it
> should cost a small amount. If it's worth a lot, it costs more, and it still leaves you the
> other 80% to 90%. If the maths doesn't work in your favour, I shouldn't be building it.

**King says:**
"That's the whole method. It means I can't quietly charge you more for a build that took me
longer, and it means I can't undercharge on something that's genuinely worth a lot to you. It
keeps both of us honest."

**Presenter note:** If they ask where the method comes from, say it plainly: it's Nate Herk's
value-based pricing approach, from AI Automation Society. That's where King learned it. Never
present it as King's own invention.

## SLIDE 10 · What that looks like in real numbers

**On screen:**

> **Worked example. Illustrative, using round numbers, not a quote.**
>
> A service business takes **20 enquiries a month.**
> Roughly **30%** of them go quiet because follow-up is slow. That's the assumption, and it's
> stated openly rather than hidden.
> Average job is worth **€200.**
>
> 20 × 30% = **6 jobs a month slipping away**
> 6 × €200 = **€1,200 a month**
> × 12 = **€14,400 a year**
>
> Project fee at 10% to 20% of that = **€1,440 to €2,880**
>
> **Even if the real figure is half of that, the build still pays for itself inside a year.**

**King says:**
"Two things about that number. First, it's an estimate, not a promise. The 30% is a common figure,
not something I've measured in your business. Second, that assumption gets replaced with your real
numbers, from your own dashboard, inside the first couple of months. That's the second of the
three things on Slide 6, and this is where you feel it."

**Presenter note:** Redo this live with their numbers if they gave you any on Slide 2. Their own
figures on the screen beat any example. If their maths comes out small, say so out loud and price
accordingly, or tell them it isn't worth building yet.

**Design:** The maths lines large and centred, gold on the three result figures only. The estimate

## APPENDIX · The questions that actually come up

Keep these as hidden slides. Pull one up only if asked.

**"How long does it take?"**
A first scoped build runs about two weeks once the agreement is signed, with progress shown
weekly. Bigger builds get broken into pieces that each go live on their own.

**"What if it doesn't work?"**
The agreement names the test it has to pass before the second half of the fee is due. If it
doesn't pass, it isn't finished, and I keep working.

**"Isn't this just ChatGPT with extra steps?"**
A chatbot waits for you to type and leaves the checking to you. This remembers your business,
runs to a schedule without you, and checks its own work before anything reaches you.

**"I'm not technical. Will I be able to use it?"**
That's who I build for. My whole audience is people who run real businesses and have no interest
in the technology. If you need to understand how it works to use it, I've built it wrong.

**"What happens if you disappear?"**
Everything runs on your accounts, in your name, with a written list of what's connected. Another
developer can pick it up. That's deliberate.

**"Can you just give me the AI tool and I'll set it up myself?"**
That's the Tier 2 idea on Slide 8 and it doesn't exist yet. Today the calibration is the service.

**"What framework do you use?"**
The build framework is WAT, and it's Nate Herk's, taught in AI Automation Society. I learned it
there and I'll point you at it happily. What's mine is the system I've built on top of it. Never
claim WAT as King's own.

**"Do you work with businesses like mine?"**
Founder-led service businesses, Ireland and the UK, mostly. Trades, salons, clinics, studios,
small agencies. If the business runs through one person's phone, it's the right shape.

## ALTERNATIVE ANGLE · The "show it first" running order

Same content, different order. Use this when the prospect is skeptical, short on time, or has
already been sold to by three AI companies this month.

**The change:** open with the working system on screen instead of with pain.

**New running order:**
1. **Title** (Slide 1, unchanged, 20 seconds)
2. **Live demo, cold, no setup.** Share the screen and click through the real seven steps, saying
   almost nothing. Let them watch a business go from intake to a captured lead in four minutes.
3. **"That took four minutes. Here's the part that matters."** Then Slide 4 (diagnose before
   build), because now they've seen the diagnosis happen rather than heard it described.
4. **Slide 2 and 3 (the four pains).** They hit harder after the demo, because the prospect is
   now mentally putting their own business into the screens they just saw.
5. **The three differences on Slide 6.** After a live demo this is the natural next beat.
6. Then Slides 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 unchanged.

**Why this version can work better:** it puts the proof before the claim. A skeptical buyer spends
the first five minutes of a normal deck waiting to find out whether you can build anything. This
answers that first and buys attention for the rest.

**Why it's the alternative and not the default:** without the pain slides first, some prospects
watch the demo and think "impressive, not for me." The default order earns the right to demo. Use
this one when you can tell in the first minute that the person wants proof before empathy.

## Notes for whoever builds the visual version

- Zero em dashes and zero en dashes anywhere in the built slides. This file is clean, keep it so.
- Do not add stock photography of people pointing at laptops. Navy, gold, type, space.
- Slide 6 can be built as two slides that both read as Slide 6. Never renumber the deck.
- Slide 8's visual hierarchy is a requirement, not a preference. Tier 1 solid and green, Tier 2
  grey and small. A screenshot of it must never read as a two-option price list.
- Slide 10's estimate caveat never gets shrunk, greyed, or moved to a footer.
- Every number on Slides 10 and 11 traces back to something real. Do not round them "for design."
