# DISCOVERY & QUESTIONING TECHNIQUES · Sourced Briefing

**Purpose:** Proven, named questioning frameworks for extracting a client's *exact* requirements with full context and zero guessing. Each section gives the core idea, the actual question patterns to steal, and when to use it. Ends with the 8, 12 highest-leverage patterns that force specifics and kill vagueness.

**Honesty note:** Everything below is from named, published frameworks (Rackham/Huthwaite, Fitzpatrick, Sandler, Toyoda/Toyota, Moesta JTBD, active-listening literature). Verbatim question lists are reproduced where the source gives them (Sandler's 8, the 5 Whys). SPIN/Mom Test/JTBD examples are representative patterns from the framework, not a single canonical script.

## 1. SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham / Huthwaite)
**Core idea:** Built on 35,000 sales calls over 12 years. You don't pitch, you ask four *escalating* question types that lead the client to articulate their own need and its value. The finding that matters: top performers spend **<15% on Situation** and **>50% on Implication + Need-payoff.** Most amateurs over-ask Situation (boring) and never build urgency.

**The four types, questions to steal:**
- **S, Situation** (context, used sparingly): "Walk me through how you currently [sell the course / handle sign-ups]." "What are you using right now to do X?" *Do your homework first so you don't waste these.*
- **P, Problem** (uncover the pain): "What's not working about your current setup?" "Where does it break down?" "Is [X] difficult / slow / costing you?"
- **I, Implication** (make the pain hurt, the urgency engine): "If sign-ups stay clunky, what does that do to your launch revenue?" "How does that knock-on affect [time / money / your reputation / next quarter]?" This takes a confirmed problem and explodes its downstream cost.
- **N, Need-payoff** (let *them* say the value): "How would it help if that were solved?" "What would a site that does X mean for you?" Gets the client selling the solution to themselves.

**When to use:** The backbone for any paid consult. Use the S→P→I→N order as the spine of the whole session.

## 2. The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)
**Core idea:** People lie to you to be nice, *especially* about your idea. The Mom Test is a set of rules for asking questions so good that *even your mom couldn't lie.* The fix: **stop talking about your solution; talk about their life and their past behaviour.**

**Three rules:**
1. Talk about **their life**, not your idea.
2. Ask about **specifics in the past**, not generics or hypotheticals about the future.
3. **Talk less, listen more.**

**Good vs bad questions (steal these):**
- BAD: "Would you pay €X for a site that does Y?" (people are optimistic and want to please you) → GOOD: "What are you using now, and what did it cost you to set up?"
- BAD: "Is price an issue?" → GOOD: "Walk me through the last time this was a problem." / "What have you already tried to fix it?"
- GOOD anchors: "Why do you bother doing it that way?" · "Talk me through your workflow." · "When was the last time this happened?"

**Commitment & advancement (the closer):** Meetings don't "go well", they either produce a **commitment** or they fail. Commitment comes in three escalating currencies: **Time → Reputation → Money.** "I'd definitely buy that" = worthless. "Here's the deposit, tell me when it's ready" = everything. Always know your ask *before* the call and make it before the call ends. **Compliments cost nothing, anchor every bit of fluff with a specific** ("When did that last happen?").

**When to use:** Whenever the client is enthusiastic but vague, and to validate that requirements are *real* (backed by past behaviour and money) rather than wishful. Critical for Olly's course site, confirm what he's *actually done/spent*, not what he says he wants.

## 3. Consultative / Solution Selling + the Sandler Pain Funnel
**Core idea:** You're a trusted advisor diagnosing before prescribing, not a vendor listing features. The Sandler **Pain Funnel** is the most concrete tool here: a fixed sequence of **8 nested questions** that takes someone from a vague gripe to a *quantified, emotionally-owned* cost in 3, 5 minutes. Crucially: **every question deepens the previous answer, it introduces no new topic, so it can't be fully pre-scripted.**

**The canonical 8 (verbatim order):**
1. "Tell me more about that."
2. "Can you be more specific? Give me an example."
3. "How long has that been a problem?"
4. "What have you tried to do about it?"
5. "Did that work?"
6. "How much do you think this has cost you?"
7. "How do you feel about that?"
8. "Have you given up trying to fix it?"

It moves through three depths: **surface complaint (1, 2) → history & prior attempts (3, 5) → quantified + emotional cost (6, 8).** The funnel is done when they name a specific cost they can't tolerate.

Pair with **open-ended discovery** (no yes/no): start with "what / how / why / tell me about / describe…" to surface root causes, not symptoms.

**When to use:** Mid-consult, the moment a client mentions any pain. Run it to convert "the current site is a bit annoying" into "the broken checkout is costing me ~€2k a launch and I've already wasted €600 on a freelancer who ghosted me."

## 4. The 5 Whys (Sakichi Toyoda / Toyota Production System)
**Core idea:** Ask "Why?" ~5 times, each aimed at the *previous answer*, to walk past the symptom to the **root cause.** A stated requirement is usually a symptom; the real requirement is 3, 5 whys down.

**Worked example (Toyota):** Car won't start → battery dead → alternator failed → belt broke → belt was past service life → *root cause:* no maintenance schedule. The fix lives at the bottom, not the top.

**Steal this in a consult:** Client: "I want a fancy animated homepage." → "Why?" → "So it looks premium." → "Why does premium matter here?" → "So people trust the course is worth €500." → "Why don't they trust it now?" → "No proof / testimonials / clear outcome." **Real requirement = a trust/proof system, not animation.** That's the build spec.

**When to use:** Any time a client hands you a *solution* ("I need X feature") instead of a *problem.* Drill until the underlying job appears, then you build the right thing.

## 5. Jobs-To-Be-Done · the Switch Interview (Bob Moesta / Chris Spiek)
**Core idea:** People "hire" a product to make progress in a specific situation. To get the true requirement, reconstruct the **timeline of their last switch**, from "first thought" to purchase, and map the **Four Forces of Progress:**
- **Push**, what's wrong with the current situation (drives them to look)
- **Pull**, the appeal of the new solution / the progress it promises
- **Anxiety**, fears about the new, unproven choice
- **Habit**, the comfort of staying put

Change happens only when **Push + Pull beat Anxiety + Habit.** ~10, 15 interviews surface most patterns; the interview structure emerges from *their story*, not your agenda.

**Questions to steal (timeline-anchored, past-tense):**
- "When did you **first** start thinking you needed a [course site]?" (first thought)
- "What was going on that made the old way stop working?" (push)
- "What did you imagine the new solution would let you do?" (pull)
- "What worried you about switching / spending the money?" (anxiety)
- "What nearly made you just stick with what you had?" (habit)
- "Walk me through the day you decided, who else was involved, what did you do first?"

**When to use:** Early, to understand *why this project, why now,* and what "good" looks like to the client emotionally, so the build sells the *progress*, not just the features. For Olly: what made him decide an FBA course needs its own site *now*, and what anxiety/habit might stall the launch.

## 6. Active Listening / Playback Confirmation
**Core idea:** The technique that gets you to **zero guessing.** You feed back what you heard in your own words so the client can correct you *before* you build. **Paraphrase mid-conversation; summarise at the end** to signal closure and lock the spec.

**Patterns to steal:**
- Paraphrase (mid): "So what you're saying is…" · "What I'm hearing is you're worried about…", then pause for correction.
- Anchor fluff: "When you say 'modern', can you point me to one site that nails it?"
- Summarise (end of section/session): "Let me read this back so I've got it exactly right: you want A, B, C; you do *not* want D; budget is E; deadline is F. What did I get wrong or miss?"

**When to use:** Continuously, and *especially* at the close of every session and every topic. This is the literal mechanism for your "let me read this back" lock, and it converts spoken answers into a written spec the client signs off on.

## THE 8-12 HIGHEST-LEVERAGE QUESTION PATTERNS
*(Force specifics, kill vagueness, pull these into your reusable template.)*

1. **"Walk me through the last time this happened."** (Mom Test, past behaviour beats opinion; kills hypotheticals)
2. **"What have you already tried, and what did it cost you?"** (Mom Test / Sandler #4, 6, reveals real effort + budget + what already failed)
3. **"Why? …and why is that?"** ×5 (5 Whys, drives a stated solution down to the real requirement)
4. **"If nothing changes, what does this cost you over the next [quarter/launch]?"** (SPIN Implication / Sandler #6, builds urgency + a number)
5. **"Can you be more specific, give me one concrete example?"** (Sandler #2, anchors every vague word)
6. **"Can you show me one site/example that nails what you mean by [premium / clean / modern]?"** (Active listening anchor, turns adjectives into a reference)
7. **"What would solving this actually let you do?"** (SPIN Need-payoff, client states the value, you don't have to argue it)
8. **"When did you first start thinking you needed this, and what triggered it?"** (JTBD, the real 'why now' + the push)
9. **"What's worried you about going ahead / spending this?"** (JTBD anxiety, surfaces hidden blockers that derail builds)
10. **"What does *done* look like to you, how will you know this worked?"** (definition-of-success / acceptance criteria, the build's pass/fail test)
11. **"What do you *not* want, anything that would be a deal-breaker?"** (negative-space scoping, catches assumptions before they're built)
12. **"Let me read this back to make sure I've got it exactly: [A, B, C; not D; budget E; deadline F]. What did I get wrong?"** (Active-listening summarise, the lock-the-spec move that feeds your WAT build prompt)

**Steerability principle to bake into the template:** after each session, sort every answer into **LOCKED** (specific + confirmed via playback) vs **OPEN** (still vague, hypothetical, or unanchored). Open items become the next session's question list. Only LOCKED items go into the build spec, that is your "zero guessing" guarantee.

## 1. Web-design / creative-brief discovery (agency practice)

The dominant agency pattern is a **5-block intake** (Content Snare's web-design intake form is a clean, representative example, these blocks repeat across ClientManager, Paperform, ManyRequests):

| Block | Why it exists | Real example questions (verbatim from the source) |
|---|---|---|
| **Company info** | anchor the brand & audience | "Can you provide a brief overview of your company and its main products or services?" · "Who is your target audience or customer base?" · "Are there any specific brand guidelines or existing visual assets we should be aware of?" |
| **Goals & functionality** | what the site must DO | "What are the primary goals and objectives for your new website?" · "What core features and functionalities do you envision (e.g., e-commerce, blog, contact forms, user registration)?" · "Do you require integration with third-party systems?" |
| **Pain points** | the real problem to solve | "What are the main challenges or pain points you currently experience with your existing website?" · "Have you received feedback or complaints from your users?" |
| **Competitor info** | calibrate the look & differentiation | "Can you provide the URLs of your competitors' websites?" · "What do you like or dislike about them?" · "Are there features you'd like to incorporate or avoid?" |
| **Timeline & budget** | constrain the build | "What is your desired timeline?" · "What is your allocated budget range?" · "Have you allocated a separate budget for ongoing maintenance?" |

**Agency rule of thumb (sourced):** keep it to **~15, 20 questions**, a ~10-question general block plus a project-specific block. More than that and clients abandon the form. (ClientManager, Content Snare.)

**The "show, don't tell" technique for look/feel** (recurring across design questionnaires): never ask a non-technical client to *describe* a style in words, they can't. Instead ask them to **point at examples**: "Share 2, 3 websites you love and tell us what specifically you like" and "Share 2, 3 you dislike." Reference-driven beats spec-driven (matches King's own `feedback_reference_driven_design.md`).

## 2. The discovery *process* layer · Double Diamond / Design Thinking

The questionnaire is just the instrument. The **framework that governs WHEN you ask and WHAT you're trying to lock** is the **Double Diamond** (British Design Council), the most-cited agency discovery model:

- **Discover**, diverge: gather everything about user needs, context, the real problem.
- **Define**, converge: turn it into ONE clear problem statement / scope.
- **Develop**, diverge: solutions, iterate.
- **Deliver**, converge: finalise and ship.

(Netguru, Future Processing.) The **priority output of the Discover→Define phases is a single well-defined problem statement** supported by personas, user goals, and constraints. (Service Innovation Lab, NUMI.)

IDEO's Design Thinking adds the human framing, *Frame a Question → Gather Inspiration → Synthesize for Action*, useful as the opening move of a session. (IDEO U.)

**Why this matters for King's "locked vs open" steerability:** the two diamonds map exactly onto his need. The first diamond is *discovery* (questions → problem statement). The handoff between diamonds, **"Define"**, is the moment you declare scope LOCKED. Everything after is build. So his session board should literally have two columns the client can see: **OPEN (still diverging)** and **LOCKED (defined)**.

## 3. Course-creator / online-course intake

No single canonical questionnaire exists here (this is honest, the course world teaches *method*, not a standard form), but the instructional-design field gives a reliable backbone built on the **ADDIE model** (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) and the **learning-outcomes** discipline. Pull the actual intake questions from these:

**Learner / audience (the "Analysis" step, do this first):**
- "Who are they, demographics, education level, how busy are they?"
- "Do they have prior knowledge of this topic?" (knowledge level)
- "What are the learner's pain points and desired goals?" (LearnWorlds learner profile)

**The transformation (the single most important course question):**
- "What will learners be able to **do** after the course that they couldn't do before?" (LearnWorlds, this is the learning-outcome test)
- Outcomes are phrased: *"By completing this course, learners will be able to…"* (U. Calgary Taylor Institute, FHSU)

**Structure:**
- "What are the modules, and what topics sit under each, in what order?" (topical outline)
- "What are the prerequisites / course requirements, if any?"
- "What assessments prove they got the result, and does each one serve a learning objective?" (assessment must map to an outcome)

**Course as a *product/site* (the business layer, from launch playbooks):**
- The offer & avatar: who is this for, what's the promise?
- Price & pricing model (one-time / subscription / tiers), set by competitive research.
- The funnel: **lead magnet → landing page → email sequence → sales page** (Thrivethemes, Thinkific). Each is a page King has to build, so each is a scope item.
- Sales page must include: benefit-led copy, instructor bio, FAQ. (Thrivethemes.)
- Platform/tech: which course host (Thinkific / Teachable / Kajabi / custom), payments, drip schedule, membership/login.

**For Olly's Amazon FBA course site specifically**, the gaps to drill will be: the FBA transformation promise, module breakdown, price + payment, the lead magnet, the sales-page proof (testimonials/results), and whether it's a hosted platform or a custom build.

## 4. Software requirements elicitation (the precision/rigour layer · BABOK & IIBA)

This is what makes the spec *unambiguous and buildable*, the discipline web/course intakes usually lack. From **BABOK (IIBA's Business Analysis Body of Knowledge)**:

**Requirement types (BABOK classification):**
- **Functional**, *what the solution must do* (features, behaviours).
- **Non-functional**, *how well it must perform* (quality attributes).
- **Transition**, temporary capabilities to get from old to new (data migration, training, deployment), often forgotten, always scope.
(Techcanvass, BABOK 10.30.)

**Non-functional requirement categories, each is a question to ask** (AltexSoft, with their example metrics):

| NFR | Question to ask | Example acceptance metric |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | How fast must it load/respond? | "Front-page load ≤ 2 seconds on LTE" |
| Scalability | How many users/how much data at peak? | "Support 200,000 concurrent users" |
| Availability | What uptime / when can it be down? | "Deployment must not exceed 1 hour or affect checkout" |
| Security | Who can access/change what? | "Only the data administrator can change permissions" |
| Usability | Who must be able to use it unaided? | "Usable by people with no technical knowledge" |
| Reliability | What happens on failure? | "Rollback all related updates if any update fails" |

**Functional vs non-functional in one line:** functional = *what it does*; non-functional = *how well it does it / its constraints*. (AltexSoft.)

**Acceptance criteria (the lock mechanism):** must be **testable, concise, and understood by all stakeholders.** Write them as a **checklist, plain text, or `Given / When / Then`** format. (BABOK; aqua-cloud.) This is King's "zero guessing" guarantee, *a requirement isn't locked until it has a test that proves it's done.*

**BABOK elicitation techniques** (the menu of HOW to extract, Ch. 4 + Ch. 10): **Interviews, Workshops, Observation, Document Analysis, Prototyping, Surveys/Questionnaires, Focus Groups, Brainstorming.** And the three tasks that give King his multi-session steerability:
1. **Prepare for Elicitation**, set objectives, pick the technique, know what you're trying to get *before* the call.
2. **Conduct Elicitation**, gather.
3. **Confirm Elicitation Results**, **validate back to the client that what you captured is accurate and complete.** This is the "read it back, lock it" step, run it at the end of every session and at the diamond handoff.

## 5. The reusable MASTER INTAKE OUTLINE (merged, with strong example questions)

This is the deliverable, one template covering all of King's services (websites, course sites, faceless AI channels, content management, automation). Each section has a **lock test** so the system always knows OPEN vs LOCKED.

**0. Context & business**
- "In one sentence, what is this and what does it need to achieve?" (the problem statement, Double Diamond Define)
- "What does success look like 90 days after launch?"

**1. Vision / goal**
- "What's the single most important outcome of this project?"
- "If we only nailed ONE thing, what would it be?"
- *Lock test:* one written sentence both sides agree on.

**2. Audience**
- "Who exactly is this for, describe one real person?"
- "What do they currently struggle with? What do they already know?"
- "Where do they come from (ads, social, search, referral)?"

**3. Offer / value** *(course + sales)*
- "What's the promise, the transformation? 'After this they can ___.'"
- "What's the price and model (one-off, subscription, tiers)?"
- "What proof do we have (testimonials, results, credentials)?"

**4. Brand / look & feel** *(reference-driven)*
- "Share 2, 3 sites/brands you love, what specifically?"
- "Share 2, 3 you dislike."
- "Existing logo, colours, fonts, assets? Or building from scratch?"

**5. Features / pages / scope** *(functional requirements)*
- "List every page/screen you need." (sitemap)
- "What must each page DO? (forms, login, payments, booking, video, e-comm)"
- "What's explicitly OUT of scope for v1?" ← *the most undervalued question; kills scope creep*
- *Lock test:* a page list + a feature list, each with one-line acceptance criteria.

**6. Content**
- "Who provides the words, images, video, you or us?"
- "Is content ready, or does it need writing/sourcing?" (biggest hidden delay)

**7. Tech / constraints** *(non-functional + integrations)*
- "What platform/host, domain, payment processor, email tool?"
- "Any integrations (CRM, calendar, course host, analytics)?"
- "Non-functionals: load speed, mobile, accessibility, who must be able to edit it themselves?"
- "Any hard constraints, existing systems, compliance, brand rules?"

**8. Funnel / journey** *(esp. course & content work)*
- "What's the path: lead magnet → landing → email → sales page → buy?"
- "What happens after they buy (onboarding, login, delivery)?"

**9. Budget**
- "Budget range for the build?"
- "Separate budget for ongoing maintenance/updates?"

**10. Timeline**
- "Target launch date? Any fixed deadline driving it?"
- "Milestones or a hard external date (campaign, event)?"

**11. Success metrics**
- "What number tells us this worked (signups, sales, time saved)?"
- *Lock test:* at least one measurable target.

**12. Risks / unknowns**
- "What could derail this? What are you unsure about?"
- "What decisions are still open on your side?"
- *Lock test:* every open item has an owner and a 'decide-by' date.

## 6. How to wire it into King's WAT system (the steerability mechanism)

Three design moves, taken straight from the frameworks above, make this run over hours/days with "zero guessing":

1. **Two-column live board (Double Diamond):** every answer lands as a line item tagged `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. The client can always see what's settled vs what's still being drilled. The Define moment = flip the whole board to LOCKED → that's the build green-light.

2. **Lock by acceptance criteria (BABOK):** nothing is `LOCKED` until it has a testable statement, checklist or `Given/When/Then`. This is the literal "zero guessing" guarantee.

3. **Confirm-Results step every session (BABOK Ch.4):** end each session by reading the locked items back and getting a yes. Open items become the agenda for next session, that's how it survives multiple sittings without re-asking.

The fully `LOCKED` board *is* the build prompt: vision + audience + offer + look + page/feature list (with acceptance criteria) + tech + funnel + metrics → drop straight into the WAT Workflow as the spec. (This also satisfies King's own Rule #13 discovery-first and his `feedback_confirm_spec_before_send.md`.)

## 1. The backbone: BABOK's 3-step elicitation cycle (this is your "session loop")

The IIBA's **BABOK Guide** (the global standard for business analysis) splits elicitation into a repeatable cycle. This is the engine you run *each session*:

1. **4.1 Prepare for Elicitation**, set the scope of *this* session, the questions, the stakeholders, the logistics.
2. **4.2 Conduct Elicitation**, actually run the session (interview, workshop, research).
3. **4.3 Confirm Elicitation Results**, **compare what you captured against the source and against everything previously captured; resolve any error or omission early.** This is the official "playback" step.

Why this matters for you: discovery is **not one meeting**. It's this 3-step loop run *as many times as needed*, with step 3 being the lock-in checkpoint every time. That's the structure that lets it span hours or days without drifting.

Sources: [BABOK 4.0 Elicitation & Collaboration](https://www.iiba.org/knowledgehub/business-analysis-body-of-knowledge-babok-guide/4-elicitation-and-collaboration/) · [4.1 Prepare](https://www.iiba.org/knowledgehub/business-analysis-body-of-knowledge-babok-guide/4-elicitation-and-collaboration/4-1-prepare-for-elicitation/) · [4.3 Confirm Elicitation Results](https://www.iiba.org/knowledgehub/business-analysis-body-of-knowledge-babok-guide/4-elicitation-and-collaboration/4-3--confirm-elicitation-results/)

> Plain-English version of 4.3 (the part most consultants skip): after every session, write up what you heard, then check it line-by-line against (a) what the client actually said and (b) everything you already had written down. If two things contradict, that's a gap, drill it next session. **The confirm step is what stops misunderstanding.**

## 2. The "playback / confirmation loop" · how the pros prevent misunderstanding

The named technique is **Confirm Elicitation Results** (BABOK 4.3 above), and in UX/research practice it's called a **"playback"** or **research playback**, you *play back* your understanding to the client and they correct it before anything gets built.

Two concrete forms used in industry:

- **Per-session playback (end of each session):** "Here's what I now believe is true. Tell me where I'm wrong." NN/g frames this as moving stakeholders from raw research output to a **shared understanding**, and warns that when findings are only *described in a document* rather than *experienced and confirmed*, stakeholders rarely internalise them. So you play it back *live and out loud*, not just email a doc. ([NN/g, Workshopping UX Research with Stakeholders](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-research-workshops/))
- **Read-back / teach-back wording (my synthesis, anchored to 4.3):** "Let me read back the locked items… [list]. Did I get any of those wrong?" Silence is not confirmation, get an explicit "yes, that's right" on each locked item.

**Your reusable rule:** *Nothing moves from OPEN to CONFIRMED without an explicit client playback-and-agree.* That single rule is the spine of "steerability."

## 3. Tracking requirement status (open vs confirmed vs parked) · the named tools

You wanted a status tag per requirement. Two real, standard tools combine to give you exactly that:

### a) Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) · the status spine
A standard RTM gives every requirement an **ID, description, source, owner, and a status field**. Honest note: the search confirmed RTMs **do carry a status column, but the standard values are things like Draft → In Progress → Approved → Completed/Verified**. "Open / Confirmed / Parked" is *not* a universal RTM standard, it's a customisation (RTM status fields are explicitly meant to be customised to your needs). So you're on solid ground using your own three-state tag, you're just not claiming it's an industry term. ([Perforce, RTM](https://www.perforce.com/resources/alm/requirements-traceability-matrix) · [project-management.com, RTM + template](https://project-management.com/requirements-traceability-matrix-rtm/))

### b) The "Parking Lot" · the named home for PARKED items
The **Parking Lot** is a classic, named facilitation tool: when something important but *not-relevant-right-now* comes up, you "park" it on a visible list to be answered later, so it isn't lost and the session stays focused. There's even a named variant, the **"assumptions parking lot"**, where you write down preconceptions at the start and return to challenge/validate them. ([SessionLab, Parking Lot](https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/parking-lot) · [Lucid Meetings, facilitation for consultants](https://blog.lucidmeetings.com/blog/facilitation-techniques-for-consultants/))

### c) Status model to actually use (my synthesis, mapping real concepts to your 3 tags)

| Your tag | Real-world equivalent | Meaning | Move-out rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **OPEN** | RTM "Draft / In Progress" | Asked but not yet answered/agreed | → CONFIRMED only via playback-and-agree |
| 🟢 **CONFIRMED** | RTM "Approved" + BABOK 4.3 confirmed | Client explicitly agreed; build-ready | Changing it later = a **change request** (§5) |
| 🟡 **PARKED** | Parking Lot item | Raised but deferred (out of scope now, or needs info/decision later) | → OPEN when its session/trigger arrives |
| ⚫ **ASSUMPTION** | Assumptions parking lot / ADR | You're proceeding *as if* true, unconfirmed | Must be confirmed or it becomes a risk |

## 4. The questioning method (so you extract requirements with ZERO guessing)

Two named frameworks give you the *actual questions*, not vague advice:

### SPIN (Neil Rackham · from analysing 35,000 sales calls)
Four question types, in order, that uncover the *real* need instead of the surface ask:
- **S, Situation:** the current reality. *Keep these to 3, 5, do your homework first* (Olly's existing setup, audience, platform).
- **P, Problem:** where it hurts now. *("What's breaking with how you sell the course today?")*
- **I, Implication:** the cost of *not* fixing it. *(Top performers ask 4× more of these.)*
- **N, Need-payoff:** make the client state the value in their own words. *("So if the site did X, what does that do for you?")*

Distribution that elite performers use: <15% on Situation, **>50% on Implication + Need-payoff**. This is how you avoid the "client gives a vague brief" trap, you don't accept the surface request, you dig to the implication. ([HubSpot, SPIN guide](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/spin-selling-the-ultimate-guide) · [Weflow, SPIN questions](https://www.weflow.ai/blog/spin-selling-questions))

### Discovery Workshop structure (named multi-session pattern)
Industry practice explicitly runs discovery as **a series of sessions with gaps**, not one marathon: e.g. *Session 1 (90 min) → a "discovery sprint" of 1, 2 weeks (you go research/draft) → Session 2 (90 min)*. The gap is where you do the BABOK 4.3 confirm + fill open items. The facilitator's job is named clearly: **"constantly clarify and summarise key points, ask probing questions to ensure shared understanding."** ([Future Processing, Discovery Workshop](https://www.future-processing.com/blog/the-discovery-workshop/) · [Lucid Meetings](https://blog.lucidmeetings.com/blog/facilitation-techniques-for-consultants/))

## 5. Scope management + change control (handling "the client changes their mind")

This is the formal answer to *steerability* and to a client who keeps moving the goalposts.

- **Baseline the scope.** Once enough is CONFIRMED, that set becomes the **scope baseline**, the approved, agreed scope you build against. ([Fiveable, Scope Control & Change Mgmt](https://fiveable.me/project-management/unit-3/scope-control-change-management/study-guide/Ei4yQ0GgLkrrqd3L))
- **Anything new after the baseline = a Change Request,** not a silent edit. The standard process: *change requested → reviewed → approved or rejected → if approved, incorporated and the baseline updated.* For a solo consultant, **you are the one-person Change Control Board**, but the *process* still protects you (it's how you avoid uncontrolled scope creep, the "uncontrolled expansion of goals/deliverables beyond the original plan"). ([Atlassian, Scope creep](https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/scope-creep) · [Xebrio, Requirements Change Management](https://xebrio.com/requirement-change-management/))
- **Decision Log / ADR pattern for "he changed his mind."** Borrow the **Architecture Decision Record** discipline: it's an **append-only log**, *if a decision changes, you don't erase the old one; you write a new record that SUPERSEDES it and link the two,* with statuses **Proposed → Accepted → Superseded.** This is gold for your use case: when Olly reverses a decision, you don't lose the history of *why* the first choice existed, you mark it Superseded and record the new one. That's what makes it fully auditable and steerable. ([adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/) · [Microsoft, ADR](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/architect-role/architecture-decision-record))

**How to handle the vague or wavering client (synthesis of the above):**
1. Vague → don't accept it; run **SPIN** down to Implication/Need-payoff until it's specific.
2. Genuinely undecided → **Park it** (it's a known unknown, not a blocker) and keep moving.
3. Changes a CONFIRMED item → it's a **Change Request**: log the old as **Superseded**, record the new, re-confirm via playback. The client *sees* the cost/impact of the change because it's tracked, not hidden.

## 6. Knowing when discovery is "DONE ENOUGH" to build

Two named tools tell you when to stop discovering and start building, so you don't over-discover or under-discover:

### MoSCoW (from DSDM / Agile Business Consortium)
Tag every CONFIRMED requirement **Must / Should / Could / Won't (this time)**. Discovery is "done enough" when **all Must-haves are CONFIRMED** (Shoulds/Coulds can still be open). DSDM's hard numeric guidance: **Must-haves should be ≤60% of total effort; keep ~20% as Coulds** as a flex buffer, anything over 60% Must is a risk to predictability. This is your objective "are we ready" gauge *and* your scope-control lever. ([Agile Business Consortium, MoSCoW](https://www.agilebusiness.org/dsdm-project-framework/moscow-prioritisation.html) · [What is MoSCoW](https://www.agilebusiness.org/resource/what-is-moscow-prioritization/))

### Definition of Ready (DoR) + INVEST (Scrum)
A requirement is build-ready when it meets a **Definition of Ready**: clear acceptance criteria, a defined user value/goal, **no unresolved dependencies**, enough detail to estimate, and small enough to build. Test each one against **INVEST**, **I**ndependent, **N**egotiable, **V**aluable, **E**stimable, **S**mall, **T**estable. ([Atlassian, Definition of Ready](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-ready) · [LogRocket, INVEST](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/writing-meaningful-user-stories-invest-principle/))

**Your "build gate" (synthesis):** Discovery is done enough to feed WAT when → **every MUST is CONFIRMED**, each Must passes the **DoR/INVEST** check (especially *Testable* + *no open dependencies*), and there are **zero OPEN items blocking a Must**. Parked Shoulds/Coulds are fine to leave; they become phase 2 or change requests.

## 7. The LIVING SPEC document (your reusable, steerable artifact)

This is the deliverable that ties every framework above together, one markdown file, owned by you, updated every session. It IS the steerability. The locked section becomes the WAT build prompt.

```markdown
# LIVING SPEC · [Client / Project]   e.g. "Olly · Amazon FBA Course Site"
Status legend: 🟢 CONFIRMED · 🔴 OPEN · 🟡 PARKED · ⚫ ASSUMPTION · ⛔ SUPERSEDED
Last updated: [date]  ·  Session #: [n]  ·  Build gate: ___ / ___ MUSTs confirmed

## 0. CONTEXT (the "why" · never lose this)
- Who the client is, who their audience is, the business goal, success metric.

## 1. REQUIREMENTS REGISTER  (the RTM · one row per requirement)
| ID | Requirement | MoSCoW | Status | Source (who said it, which session) | Confirmed? (playback date) | Notes / dependencies |
|----|-------------|--------|--------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------|----------------------|
| R1 | Course delivered as drip-fed modules | Must | 🟢 | Olly, S1 | ✔ 22-Jun | none |
| R2 | Payment via Stripe + one-off + payment plan | Must | 🔴 | Olly, S1 | · | needs Olly's Stripe answer |
| R3 | Affiliate/referral program | Could | 🟡 PARKED | Olly, S1 | · | revisit phase 2 |
| R4 | Membership login area | Must | ⚫ ASSUMPTION | inferred | · | CONFIRM next session |

## 2. PARKING LOT  (raised, deferred · nothing gets lost)
- Affiliate program (R3), phase 2
- "Maybe a community forum?", needs Olly decision

## 3. DECISION LOG  (append-only · handles changed minds)
| # | Decision | Date | Status | Supersedes | Why |
|---|----------|------|--------|-----------|-----|
| D1 | Platform = custom site (not Kajabi) | S1 | Accepted | · | cost + control |
| D2 | Use Kajabi after all | S2 | Accepted | ⛔ D1 | Olly wants built-in hosting |

## 4. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR NEXT SESSION  (the drill list)
- R2: which Stripe model? one-off, plan, or both?
- Confirm R4 (membership area) assumption.

## 5. BUILD-GATE CHECK
- [ ] All MUSTs 🟢 CONFIRMED
- [ ] Each MUST passes DoR/INVEST (testable, no open dependency)
- [ ] No OPEN item blocks a MUST
→ When all ticked: COPY the 🟢 CONFIRMED rows → becomes the WAT build prompt.
```

**Why this is "steerability at its finest":** at any moment, one glance tells you (a) what's locked, (b) what's still open and must be drilled, (c) what's parked, (d) every decision and every reversal with its reason, and (e) exactly how close you are to the build gate. The confirmed rows drop straight into the W of WAT as the spec.

## 8. One-line summary of the method (the reusable system)

> **Run BABOK's Prepare → Conduct → Confirm loop each session; ask in SPIN order to kill vagueness; tag every requirement in a Living Spec as OPEN/CONFIRMED/PARKED/ASSUMPTION (RTM + Parking Lot); promote OPEN→CONFIRMED only by live playback; log every decision append-only with Superseded status (ADR) so changed minds are tracked not lost; baseline the scope and treat new asks as Change Requests; and declare "done enough to build" when every MoSCoW MUST is CONFIRMED and passes Definition-of-Ready/INVEST.**
