# Sin, the Passions & Repentance · When Is Something Really a Sin? (vaping, THC, the body, and the fear of "using God") · 🕊️ Formation + Theology

**Date:** 2026-06-27  ·  **Track:** Formation + Theology (the moral life, born from King's own questions)

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King asked the real questions, the honest ones: can something "bad" sometimes not be bad, depending on the situation? Is my vape a sin? What about the THC, or cigarettes, or snus? Can a person stay disciplined and still keep doing a few of these and still follow Christ? And the deepest one of all: if I keep repenting but keep falling, am I just being snaky, using God to get into heaven?

Here is the heart of it in baby words. Sin is not first about breaking rules. The word the Bible uses for sin means "missing the mark," like an arrow aimed at God that lands somewhere else. So the real test of almost anything is simple: is this pulling me toward God, or away from Him? Is it serving me, or is it starting to own me? Some things are wrong by their very nature, and no situation makes them right. But a lot of ordinary things (your phone, food, running, even studying) are good, and only go wrong when they start to rule you or pull you away from God and people.

And here is the freeing truth at the end. A man who keeps getting up after he falls is not "using God." That getting-up is what following Christ actually looks like. The goal was never to sneak past the gate into heaven. The goal is Him. And you said that yourself: "sin is away from Him, that's all." That one line is the whole answer.

## In one line
Sin is distance from the God you love, not just rule-breaking, so the fight is never "how much can I keep" but "let nothing own me except Christ," and getting up again after every fall is the path, not the cheat.

## The lesson

**1. What sin actually is.** The Bible's word for sin (*hamartia*) is an archery word: it means "missing the mark." So sin is not first a list of forbidden things. It is an arrow meant for God that goes somewhere else. That changes the whole question. The thing to ask of anything is not only "is it on the naughty list," but "is this drawing me toward God or away from Him, and is it serving me or starting to rule me?" And repentance (*metanoia*) means "a change of mind, a turning back." It is not a magic word. It is turning your face back toward God.

**2. Two kinds of "bad."** This answers King's first question (can bad sometimes not be bad).
- **Bucket A: wrong by its very nature.** The act itself misses the mark no matter the situation, so no situation rehabilitates it (for example adultery, fornication, getting drunk to escape).
- **Bucket B: good or neutral things that go wrong through disorder.** Food, rest, the phone, running, study. None are sins. God gave us bodies, work, rest, even pleasure. They "become sin" when one of three things happens: they start to **master** you, they **pull you away** from God and people, or they become an **escape or an idol**. The Fathers call a disordered desire like this a **passion** (not "passion" as in enthusiasm, but a hook in you that drags you around). Gluttony is just food gone disordered. Sloth is rest gone disordered.

The test for Bucket B is straight from Scripture: *"All things are lawful for me, but all things are not helpful. All things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any"* (1 Corinthians 6:12, NKJV). The line is not pleasure. The line is **power**. Does it bring me under its power?

**3. Sobriety of mind, and the difference between use and abuse.** The Church prizes *nepsis*, which means **watchfulness, a clear and sober mind before God**: *"Be sober, be vigilant, because your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion"* (1 Peter 5:8). This is why the Church never banned wine but always condemned **drunkenness**. Wine is blessed (Christ's first miracle made wine at Cana; wine becomes His Blood in the Eucharist; *"wine makes glad the heart of man,"* Psalm 103:15 in the OSB Septuagint, 104:15 in the NKJV). What is condemned is losing the clear mind: *"Do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation"* (Ephesians 5:18, NKJV).

**4. Vaping, THC, cigarettes, snus, sorted honestly.** There are two separate questions, and mixing them up is what makes it confusing:
- **Question 1, does it cloud the mind?** (the sobriety question, 1 Peter 5:8)
- **Question 2, does it harm or enslave the body?** (*"your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you... therefore glorify God in your body,"* 1 Corinthians 6:19-20, NKJV)

Sorting them:
- **Cigarettes / smoking tobacco:** does not cloud the mind, but it is the worst on the body axis (combustion, proven disease, hard addiction). This is why many Orthodox spiritual fathers actively discourage smoking, and it is not permitted on Mount Athos. Not a "dogmatic sin," but clearly a harmful passion to be free of.
- **Snus / nicotine pouches** and **nicotine vaping:** do not cloud the mind, less bodily harm than smoking, but still nicotine addiction. A Bucket B passion to watch.
- **THC (vaped or smoked):** the only one that hits **both** axes. It alters the mind by design (Question 1), and if smoked it also burns the body (Question 2).

A note on the wine comparison King raised ("THC is like drinking for me, moderate, doesn't make me drunk-minded"). The honest catch is that the analogy borrows wine's blessed place. Wine in moderation does not alter perception; that is why the Church can bless it. THC's whole mechanism, even moderate, is psychoactive, the mind-change is the function, not a side effect. So the fair question is not "am I fully drunk," it is whether a small altering of the clear mind is itself the thing sobriety guards.

**5. "Maybe I medically need it."** Take this seriously, because the Church does. The Church loves medicine. Scripture itself says God put medicines in the earth: *"Honour a physician according to your need of him, for the Lord created him... The Lord created medicines out of the earth, and a sensible man will not loathe them"* (the sense of Sirach 38:1-4, the Wisdom of Sirach in the OSB Old Testament). St Luke who wrote a Gospel was *"the beloved physician"* (Colossians 4:14), and the Church venerates the **Holy Unmercenary physicians** (Sts Cosmas, Damian, Panteleimon). So if a real medical need is treated, prescribed and supervised, that is **medicine, not sin**, even a mind-altering substance (the Church gives morphine to the dying).

But there is an honest line between **prescribed-and-supervised medicine** and **self-medication**. "It helps me" is true of almost everything (alcohol "helps" stress, scrolling "helps" boredom), so "it helps" is the costume an attachment wears to look respectable. The clean way to settle "do I medically need it" is not to decide it yourself (we are all biased toward what we already like) and not to let a study aid decide it. **Ask a doctor:** is there a real condition, and is this the right treatment? A yes under a doctor's care is blessed. If no doctor would prescribe it, then "medical" is doing a job the conscience already suspects.

**6. Sex and masturbation.** Sexual union is Bucket A. The Church teaches it is God's gift for marriage, the one-flesh covenant of husband and wife (*"the two shall become one flesh,"* Genesis 2:24; Christ repeats it, Matthew 19:4-6). "Safe sex" guards the body from consequences, but it does not change what the act is or where it belongs, so it does not change the moral question for the Church: *"Flee sexual immorality"* (1 Corinthians 6:18, NKJV). Masturbation the tradition treats the same way, as part of the passion of lust to be healed. This is not a place for condemnation or heavy burdens, it is a place for the hospital of Confession and for Fr Bogdan's gentle guidance, the way a physician treats a patient.

**7. The big one: is repenting-but-not-stopping just "using God"?** King feared it was snaky, like using grace as a loophole. Three truths:
- **The danger he fears is real and the Church names it.** *"Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not!"* (Romans 6:1-2, NKJV). So the instinct that something would be wrong about gaming grace is a true instinct.
- **But the very fact that it grieves him proves he is not doing it.** A person coldly using God to game the system does not lie awake grieved about gaming the system. The grief is the Holy Spirit at work. Presumption is cold ("I will sin freely, grace covers it"). Repentance is what King has ("I keep falling though I truly do not want to, and it kills me, and I get up"). From outside they look alike. In the heart they are opposites, and the grief tells which one he is.
- **Falling and rising is the path, and the Fathers are not embarrassed by it.** *"For a righteous man may fall seven times and rise again"* (Proverbs 24:16, the sense in both the NKJV and the OSB Septuagint). It calls the man who fell seven times **righteous**. St John Climacus built the whole *Ladder of Divine Ascent* assuming you fall off rungs; the saints are not the ones who never fell, they are the ones who refused to stay down. And the Father in the parable *runs* to meet the returning son every time (Luke 15:20). There is *"now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus"* (Romans 8:1, NKJV).

**8. The reframe (King's own breakthrough).** Most people, even religious people, think of sin as rule-breaking that earns punishment, and heaven as the reward for behaving. King said it the deep way: sin is **distance from the Person you love**, and heaven is **being with Him**. That dissolves the "am I using God" fear by itself. If the goal were the reward, repenting-without-stopping would be gaming it. But if the goal is **Him**, then every time you turn back you are not playing a system, you are coming home. Aim at Christ, and the passions loosen as a side effect of getting closer, instead of white-knuckling a list.

## Two tests to bring real answers (not arguments)
- **The fasting test.** The Church regularly fasts from good, lawful things (meat, wine, oil) to prove we are free. So: could you go all of Lent, forty days, with zero of the thing, easily, no ache, no counting days? Easy yes is strong evidence it does not own you. A flinch is the honest answer about who serves whom. Test it, do not just reason it.
- **The altar test.** You can lift a glass of wine to God and say "bless this," and He does, it is in the Liturgy. Can you hold the thing up the same way, would you do it right before Communion, would you do it and then pray for an hour? Whatever your gut does when you picture that is more honest than any argument.

## The seven lenses (the deep read)
- **The Why:** God made a world full of powerful things, and almost every one of them can heal or harm depending on dose, purpose, and who is in control (the poppy gives both morphine and heroin). The existence of a powerful thing is never God's blessing on every use of it. So the moral life is not a list, it is the freedom to use every good thing rightly, owned by nothing but Christ.
- **Lived Tradition:** this is the ground of **fasting** (proving we are free, the fasting test above), of **Confession** (the hospital, not the courtroom, where the struggle is laid down and healed), and of the **Jesus Prayer** (turning the face back to Christ in the moment of the pull). The personal pace of fasting and the handling of Confession are **Fr Bogdan's** to set.
- **Connections:** ties straight to the lesson "Slave to Sin, Free in Christ" (Romans 6 to 8, the war within), to "Humility, the Accuser & Discerning Your Thoughts" (telling godly sorrow from despairing shame), and to Matthew 4 (Christ beats temptation with the Word, fasting, and a clear mind, the same weapons offered here).

## The backing (verified)
- **Scripture (NKJV for NT, verify via Bible Gateway):** 1 Corinthians 6:12; 6:18; 6:19-20; 1 Peter 5:8; Ephesians 5:18; Romans 6:1-2; Romans 8:1; Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:4-6; Luke 15:20; Colossians 4:14.
- **OSB Septuagint (flag the differences):** Psalm 103:15 (LXX numbering; = 104:15 in the NKJV/Hebrew) on wine gladdening the heart; Proverbs 24:16 ("falls seven times and rises") given by sense, matching both NKJV and the Septuagint.
- **Sirach 38:1-4** (the Wisdom of Sirach, in the Orthodox Old Testament) on honouring the physician and God making medicines from the earth, given **by sense** (wording varies by edition, substance verified, not a fabricated direct quote).
- **Tradition:** St John Climacus, *The Ladder of Divine Ascent* (the falling-and-rising ascetic life, general reference, no invented quote); the Holy Unmercenary physicians (Sts Cosmas, Damian, Panteleimon); the ascetic discouragement of smoking (Mount Athos), stated as pastoral practice, not dogma.
- **Greek terms:** *hamartia* (missing the mark), *metanoia* (change of mind, turning back), *nepsis* (watchfulness, sobriety of mind), standard, verified.

## Live it this week
1. Pick the one thing pressing hardest (likely the THC, because of the mind question) and run the **forty-day fast test** on it. Do not argue it, test it, and notice honestly what happens.
2. Do the **altar test** in your heart once this week before you reach for it.
3. Keep one shield ready for the pull: the **Jesus Prayer**, *"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner."*
4. Take the whole of this to **Fr Bogdan and to Confession**, honestly, as a patient walking into a hospital (no condemnation, Romans 8:1). And for the "do I medically need it" question, **ask a doctor**, so you can tell real medicine from self-medicating.
