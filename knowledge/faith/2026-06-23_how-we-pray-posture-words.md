# How Should We Pray? Posture, Words, and the Heart
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** Lived Tradition / Formation (the Orthodox Way) · *born from King's own question*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King asked: when we pray, does it matter how we pray, our posture, how we speak, what we say? The Church's answer is: **the heart matters most, and the body matters too, and the two work together.** God is not impressed by clever or fancy words. He looks at the heart, so the most important things are humility and being honest with Him (remember the tax collector who just said "God, be merciful to me a sinner" and went home right with God, while the show-off Pharisee did not). At the same time, Orthodoxy never treats the body as unimportant, because we are body and soul together, not just souls. So we pray with our whole selves: we stand, we make the sign of the cross, we bow, sometimes we go all the way down (prostrations), we pray in front of icons, often facing east. None of that is magic and none of it earns God's ear. It does something for *us*: it gathers our wandering mind, humbles us, and pulls our whole person into the prayer. On *what* to say, we use both the set prayers the Church gives us (the Our Father, the morning and evening prayers, the Psalms, and above all the Jesus Prayer) and our own words straight from the heart. This matters for King because it frees him: he never has to find the perfect words, he just has to come honestly, and the posture and the set prayers are there to help him, not to trap him.

## The lesson

**1. The heart comes first.** God looks at the heart, not the polish of the words. Christ warns against praying to be seen and against piling up words to be impressive: *"And when you pray, do not use vain repetitions as the heathen do. For they think that they will be heard for their many words"* (Matthew 6:7, NKJV). And the parable of the two men praying settles it: the Pharisee gave a grand prayer about himself; the tax collector *"would not so much as raise his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, 'God, be merciful to me a sinner!'"* (Luke 18:13, NKJV). It was the humble one who went home justified. Sincerity and humility beat eloquence every time.

**2. But the body matters too, because we are body and soul.** Orthodoxy is deeply embodied. We are not just souls, so we pray with the whole person. That is why the Church gives us posture and gesture: standing for prayer, the **sign of the cross**, bowing, **prostrations** (going down low, especially in repentance), praying before **icons**, often **facing east**, lighting a candle. This is not magic and it does not buy God's attention. It gathers a scattered mind, it humbles the body along with the heart, and it stops prayer from being only a thought in the head.

**3. What to say: both kinds of prayer.** Two good kinds, and we use both:
- **The Church's set prayers** (the Our Father, the morning and evening prayers, the Psalms, and above all the **Jesus Prayer**: *"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"*). These teach us how to pray and carry us when we have no words of our own.
- **Our own words**, plainly, like talking to a Father. Telling God the real thing, the worry, the thanks, the sorrow. That is prayer too.

**4. Pray always, even briefly.** *"Pray without ceasing"* (1 Thessalonians 5:17, NKJV). A short prayer through the day, the Jesus Prayer under your breath, keeps the line to God open. It does not all have to happen in one long sitting.

**5. The shape of a daily rule is Fr Bogdan's part.** The *principle* (pray with a humble heart, with your body, with set and personal prayer) is the Church's. The *shape* of your own daily rule (how long, which prayers, when) is what a spiritual father sets. Keep it small and keepable: two faithful minutes every day beats twenty you dread and quit. Bring your actual rule to Fr Bogdan and let him set the pace.

## Live it this week
Tonight, stand before an icon, make the sign of the cross, say the Our Father slowly, then speak to God plainly in your own words about whatever is on your heart (the loan, the worry, your thanks), and end with the Jesus Prayer a few times. Do not wait to feel ready or to find the right words. Come honestly. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back. Ask Fr Bogdan to set you a small daily rule so this becomes a steady rhythm, not a one-off.

## Prayer
