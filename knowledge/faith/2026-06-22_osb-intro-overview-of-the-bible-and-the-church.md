## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
Think of the Bible not as one book but as a whole **library**, many books, written over a very long time, that the Church gathered together. It comes in two big halves called **Testaments** ("Testament" just means a covenant, a binding promise between God and His people): the **Old Testament** is everything before Jesus, and the **New Testament** is Jesus and after. Inside each half, the books are sorted by type, for the Old it's Law, History, Wisdom (like prayers and poems), and Prophecy (God's messengers); for the New it's the four Gospels (the life of Jesus told four ways), Acts (the early Church's story), the Letters (teaching to young churches), and Revelation (a vision of the end and heaven's worship). The second part explains *whose* Bible this is: it belongs to the **Church**, and "Orthodox" means "right belief / right worship", the original Church Jesus started, kept unbroken for 2,000 years. We know God through this Church not by Scripture alone but together with her worship, her teachers (the Fathers), her councils, icons, and saints, all one **Holy Tradition**. This matters for King because it gives him a map: he is reading the same Bible, inside the same Church, that he has joined through his own parish under Fr Bogdan.

## PART A · Overview of the Books of the Bible (the map)
The Bible is not one book but a **library**, many books, written over ~1,500 years, gathered by the
Church into two Testaments ("Testament" = covenant, a binding agreement between God and His people). The
**Old Testament** = before Christ; the **New Testament** = Christ and after. They are grouped by *type*.

### THE OLD TESTAMENT · four kinds of books
1. **The Law (the Pentateuch), the 5 books of Moses:** **Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers,
   Deuteronomy.** The beginning: creation, the fall, God choosing Abraham, the Exodus from Egypt, and the
   Law given through Moses. *(These contain the previews of Christ we'll keep meeting, the Passover lamb,
   the manna, Melchizedek.)*
2. **The Books of History:** Joshua, Judges, Ruth, the Kingdoms (1, 2 Samuel & 1, 2 Kings), Chronicles,
   Ezra/Nehemiah, Esther, and in the Orthodox Bible also **Tobit, Judith, and the Maccabees.** The story
   of God's people in the Promised Land: the judges, the kings (David!), the exile, the return.
3. **The Books of Wisdom (and poetry):** **Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, the Song of Songs**, and
   in the Orthodox Bible also **the Wisdom of Solomon and the Wisdom of Sirach.** Prayer, poetry, and how
   to live well before God. (The **Psalms** are the Church's prayer book, you prayed Psalm 51 this morning.)
4. **The Books of Prophecy:** the **Major Prophets** (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Baruch, Ezekiel,
   Daniel) and the **Minor Prophets** (Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk,
   Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi). God's messengers calling the people back, and foretelling
   Christ (e.g. **Isaiah 7:14**, "a virgin shall conceive… Emmanuel" — which we met in Matthew 1).

> **Note on the Orthodox Old Testament:** the Orthodox Church uses the **Septuagint**, the ancient Greek
> translation the Apostles themselves quoted, so it includes a few more books than a Protestant Bible
> (like Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, the Maccabees). This is part of "the Bible is the Church's book."

### THE NEW TESTAMENT · five kinds of books (27 in all)
1. **The Four Gospels:** **Matthew, Mark, Luke, John**, the life, teaching, death, and resurrection of
   Jesus, told four ways. *(We've begun Matthew.)*
2. **The Acts of the Apostles:** the history of the early Church, Pentecost and how the faith spread.
   *(This is the "Acts", our lesson #5, Pentecost, is from here.)*
3. **The Epistles (letters) of St Paul**, 14 letters teaching the young churches: Romans,
   1, 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1, 2 Thessalonians, 1, 2 Timothy, Titus,
   Philemon, and **Hebrews.**
4. **The General (Catholic) Epistles**, 7 letters from other Apostles: **James, 1, 2 Peter, 1, 3 John,
   Jude.** *(1 Peter 3:15, our "give a defense" verse, is here.)*
5. **The Revelation (the Apocalypse) of St John**, the vision of the end, Christ's victory, and the
   worship of heaven. *(Its "Holy, Holy, Holy" around God's throne is what we sing in the Liturgy.)*

## PART B · Introducing the Orthodox Church (whose Bible this is)
The OSB opens by introducing the Church, because **the Bible is the Church's book**, read rightly only
from inside her. The key points:
- **"Orthodox"** means **"right belief / right worship / right glory"** (from Greek *orthos*, "right," +
  *doxa*, "glory/belief"). To be Orthodox is to glorify God rightly, as the Church always has.
- **The Orthodox Church is the original Church**, the one Christ founded and the Apostles spread (Acts),
  continuing **unbroken for 2,000 years.** It is not a denomination that branched off; it is the trunk.
- **It holds "the faith once delivered to the saints"** (Jude 1:3) — guarded by the **Seven Ecumenical
  Councils** (AD 325, 787) and the **Church Fathers**, without adding to it or taking away.
- **How we know God in this Church:** Scripture **and** the Liturgy, the sacraments, the Fathers, the
  Councils, the icons and the saints, all one **Holy Tradition** (see the recap note for the fuller list).
- **It is the same Church** of the martyrs, the Fathers, and the saints, and the same Church King has
  entered through his parish (**St Brendan & St Joseph, Romanian Orthodox, Dublin**), under **Fr Bogdan.**
