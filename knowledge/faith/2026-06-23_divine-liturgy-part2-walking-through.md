# How the Divine Liturgy Works · Part 2: Walking Through It
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** ② The Tradition · the Divine Liturgy (St John Chrysostom)

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
The **Divine Liturgy** is the main worship service of the Orthodox Church, and this note walks through it like a journey from start to finish. It has two big parts. First is the **Liturgy of the Word**: we open by stepping "into the Kingdom of God," we pray together for the whole world, the Gospel book is carried in like Jesus coming out to teach, and then God's words are read aloud to His people. Second is the **Liturgy of the Eucharist** (the special meal): bread and wine are carried to the altar, everyone says together what they believe, and we "lift up our hearts" to heaven and sing the very song the angels sing around God's throne. Then comes the holy heart of it all, the **epiclesis**, where the priest asks the **Holy Spirit** (the same Spirit that came at Pentecost) to come down and change the bread and wine into the real **Body and Blood of Christ**, which the people then receive in **Communion**. The Church calls this "heaven on earth", not just thinking about God, but actually joining the angels and meeting Christ Himself (this feeds **theosis**, being filled with God's life). This matters for King because it's where everything he studies actually *happens*; he is meant to go, stand in it, and let Fr Bogdan guide how he prepares to receive Communion.

## In one line
The Liturgy is one great movement: we gather into God's Kingdom, hear His Word, lift our hearts to heaven, the Holy Spirit comes down and makes the bread and wine the very Body and Blood of Christ, and we receive Him, it is **heaven on earth.**

## The lesson · walking through the Liturgy
*(Two great parts: the **Liturgy of the Word**, for everyone, and the **Liturgy of the Eucharist**, the offering and Communion.)*

**Part one, the Word:**
- **"Blessed is the Kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit…"**, the very first words. We don't begin an ordinary meeting; we step **into the Kingdom of God.**
- **The Great Litany**, *"In peace let us pray to the Lord."* We pray, together, for the whole world.
- **The Little Entrance**, the Gospel book is carried in procession: a picture of **Christ coming forth to teach.**
- **The Epistle and the Gospel** (and the homily), **God speaks to His people.**

**Part two, the Eucharist:**
- **The Cherubic Hymn + the Great Entrance**, the bread and wine are carried to the altar while we sing that we *"who mystically represent the Cherubim"* now lay aside all earthly cares. **Heaven and earth join**; we stand with the angels.
- **The Creed**, together we confess the **one faith** ("I believe in one God…").
- **The Anaphora (the great thanksgiving)**, the heart of the offering:
  - *"Let us lift up our hearts."*, *"We lift them up unto the Lord."* (Everything now ascends.)
  - **The Sanctus:** *"Holy, holy, holy, Lord of Sabaoth"* — the very song of the angels around God's throne (**Isaiah 6:3**). We are singing *with* heaven.
  - **The words of Christ:** *"Take, eat; this is my body… this is my blood"* (Luke 22:19; Matt 26:26–28).
- **The Epiclesis**, the priest calls down the **Holy Spirit** upon the gifts to change the bread and wine into the **Body and Blood of Christ.** This is the holy heart of the whole Liturgy, the same Spirit of Pentecost, come again.
- **The Lord's Prayer**, we pray as Christ taught us.
- **Holy Communion**, *"With the fear of God, faith, and love, draw near."* We receive **Christ Himself**, the medicine of immortality, union with God (*theosis* fed).
- **The Dismissal**, *"Let us depart in peace"*, sent back into the world carrying Christ.

## The lenses
- **Lived Tradition / formation:** this is *where* everything we study actually happens, theosis is fed here.
- **Beauty:** we don't just think about God; we **join the angels' song**, the Liturgy is heaven's beauty on earth (recall Prince Vladimir's envoys: *"we knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth"*).
- **Connections:** the Last Supper (Luke 22), Isaiah's vision (Isaiah 6), Pentecost (the Spirit descending), and Acts 2:42's four marks all meet here.
- **Theology:** the gifts truly become Christ's Body and Blood by the Spirit, not a symbol only, but real presence.
