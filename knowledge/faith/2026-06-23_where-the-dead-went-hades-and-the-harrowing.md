# Where the Dead Went Before Christ: Hades and the Harrowing of Hades
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** 🧠 Theology + ⛪ Lived Tradition (Pascha) · *born from King's question*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King asked what death meant for humans before Jesus. Before Christ, everyone who died, the good and the bad alike, went down to a place the Hebrews called Sheol and the Greeks called Hades, simply "the place of the dead." It was a shadowy holding place, separated from the full life and joy of God, because the gates of heaven had been shut by sin and death. Even the great righteous ones, Abraham, Moses, King David, went there, waiting in hope, because no one yet could open the door back to God. Death was a one-way trip down, like a locked dungeon under the world. Then Jesus died, and something earth-shaking happened: His soul went DOWN into Hades, but because He is Life and God Himself, death could not hold Him. He smashed the gates of Hades from the inside, destroyed death's power, and led out everyone who had been waiting. This is called the Descent into Hades, or the Harrowing of Hades, and it is the great icon of Orthodox Easter (Pascha): Christ standing on the broken gates, pulling Adam and Eve up out of their tombs by the wrists. So after Christ, death is changed forever. For those in Christ it is no longer a prison but a "falling asleep," a passage to be with the Lord, while we await the resurrection of the body at the end. This matters for King because it means death has already been beaten; for a Christian it is now a door, not a dead end.

## In one line
Before Christ all the dead, even the righteous, went down to Hades (Sheol), the shadowy realm of the dead with heaven's gates shut; then Christ descended into death, shattered it from within, and led the waiting righteous out, turning death from a locked dungeon into a doorway.

## The teaching

**1. Before Christ: everyone went down to Hades / Sheol.** Sheol (Hebrew) and Hades (Greek) both mean the realm of the dead. The Old Testament pictures all the dead going *down* there. Jacob: *"I shall go down into the grave (Sheol) to my son in mourning"* (Genesis 37:35). It was a dim place of separation, where one could not praise God as in life: *"For in death there is no remembrance of You; in the grave who will give You thanks?"* (Psalm 6:5, NKJV). The gates of heaven, full union with God, were not yet open, because sin and death barred the way.

**2. Even the righteous waited there, in hope.** Abraham, Moses, David, the prophets, all died and went to await their deliverance. Jesus' own parable shows a distinction within the realm of the dead: the poor man Lazarus is carried *"to Abraham's bosom"* (a place of comfort), while the rich man is *"in torments in Hades"* (Luke 16:22-23, NKJV). The righteous rested in hope; none had yet entered the fullness of heaven, because the Door had not yet come.

**3. Then Christ descended into death itself (the Harrowing of Hades).** When Christ died, His soul went down to Hades, but as Life and God He could not be held: *"You will not leave my soul in Hades, nor will You allow Your Holy One to see corruption"* (Acts 2:27, NKJV, quoting Psalm 15/16). *"...it was not possible that He should be held by it"* (Acts 2:24). Scripture: *"He went and preached to the spirits in prison"* (1 Peter 3:19); *"the gospel was preached also to those who are dead"* (1 Peter 4:6); *"He also descended first into the lower parts of the earth"* (Ephesians 4:9). He broke the gates and led the captives out.

**4. This is the very heart of Pascha (Orthodox Easter).** The great Paschal hymn sings it: *"Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life."* The Pascha icon is not Jesus floating from a tomb; it is Christ standing on the shattered gates of Hades, gripping Adam and Eve by the wrists and pulling them up. Death entered to swallow a man and met God, and choked on Life.

**5. What death means now, after Christ.** For those in Christ, death is transformed from a dead end into a doorway. The New Testament now calls it *"falling asleep"* (which is why we call the departed those who have "fallen asleep," and a "cemetery" comes from the Greek for a sleeping place). *"I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live"* (John 11:25, NKJV). To die in Christ is *"to be absent from the body and to be present with the Lord"* (2 Corinthians 5:8). The gates of paradise are reopened (the thief on the cross: *"today you will be with Me in Paradise,"* Luke 23:43). The full and final victory is the **resurrection of the body** at the end of the age (1 Corinthians 15), when death itself is destroyed forever: *"O Death, where is your sting?"* (1 Corinthians 15:55).

## The backing (verified)
- Genesis 37:35 (Sheol); Psalm 6:5 (no praise in the grave); Luke 16:22-23 (Abraham's bosom / Hades); Luke 23:43 (Paradise today) (NKJV).
- Acts 2:24, 27, 31 (Christ not held by Hades, quoting Psalm 15/16 LXX); 1 Peter 3:18-19; 1 Peter 4:6; Ephesians 4:9-10 (the descent).
- John 11:25; 2 Corinthians 5:8; 1 Corinthians 15:54-55 (death now a sleep and a passage; final resurrection).
- The Paschal troparion: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life." (standard liturgical text)
- The Anastasis (Harrowing of Hades) icon as the Orthodox image of the Resurrection.

## Live it this week
When death or the fear of it touches your mind (your own or a loved one's), pray the Paschal line: *"Christ is risen, trampling down death by death."* For a Christian, death is now a sleep in the arms of the One who already broke it.

## Prayer
*O Christ our God, You went down into death and shattered its gates, and You led the captives out into life. Take from me the fear of death, for You have made it a doorway to You. Remember me in Your Kingdom. Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner. Amen.*
