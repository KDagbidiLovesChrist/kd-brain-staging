# Confession · the Mystery of Repentance (my first confession, the enemy at the door, and being received by Chrismation) · ⛪ Lived Tradition + 🕊️ Formation

**Date:** 2026-06-27  ·  **Track:** Lived Tradition (the Mysteries) + Formation (born from King's first Confession, waiting in the church)

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King was sitting in church for his **first Confession**. Fr Bogdan had told him to come. It was quiet, nothing like the Liturgy, just the priest speaking softly with one person at a time. And while he waited, everything inside him went to war: thoughts flooding, a strong pull to *just leave*, hunger gnawing, a sudden suspicion about who the priest greeted, and then a heavy sleepiness. He asked the honest question: is this the enemy, or is this God, or am I just tired?

Here is the heart of it in baby words. Confession is not a group event and it is not a punishment, it is **one person, the priest as a witness, and Christ doing the healing.** It is a hospital, not a courtroom. And the reason the fight got so loud right at the door is that the enemy fights hardest right before a soul is about to be set free. The pull to leave was not a sign King was doing it wrong, it was a sign he was doing it *right*. Staying in the seat was already repentance.

He also learned two big, freeing things. First, that the deep cry he feels when life crushes him, *"only God can save me"*, and the tears that come with it, are not weakness; the Church calls that a **gift**, the grace that pulls a person toward God. Second, that because he was **baptised as a Catholic**, he is not starting from zero, he can be **received into the Orthodox Church by Chrismation** (being sealed with the Holy Spirit), and that opens the door to Holy Communion in due order. The enemy fought so hard at the door precisely because *this* was on the other side of it.

## In one line
Confession is the hospital where Christ heals you through your spiritual father; the enemy fights hardest right at its door, so staying is already victory, and for a man already baptised, Chrismation is the door home to the Chalice.

## The lesson

**1. What Confession actually is (the Mystery of Repentance).** In the Orthodox Church, Confession is one of the **Mysteries** (Sacraments). It is **one person at a time, face to face**, which is why it is so quiet and feels nothing like the Liturgy. The Liturgy is the whole Body together, sung and public; Confession is personal and still. Crucially, **you do not confess *to* the priest, you confess to Christ, with the priest as a witness.** The rite itself says this in sense: *"I am only a witness… Christ stands here invisibly and receives your confession."* The priest is a trusted spiritual father and physician of souls who hears it and pronounces *God's* forgiveness (the absolution). It is grounded in Scripture:
- *"Confess your trespasses to one another… that you may be healed"* (James 5:16, NKJV) — confession is for **healing**.
- The risen Christ breathed on the apostles: *"Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them"* (John 20:22-23, NKJV) — He gave the Church real authority to forgive.
- *"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness"* (1 John 1:9, NKJV).

**It is a hospital, not a courtroom.** You walk in sick and walk out healed, not a defendant being sentenced, a patient being treated.

**2. Why the fight got loud right at the door (acedia / the noonday demon).** The flood of "leave, later, this is pointless," the restlessness, the hunger gnawing harder, the sudden suspicion about who the priest greeted — the Fathers have a name for this whole cluster: **acedia** (listlessness / despondency), what the desert monks called **the noonday demon** (from *"the destruction that wastes at noonday,"* Psalm 90:6 in the OSB Septuagint; 91:6 in the NKJV). Evagrius and St John Cassian (*Institutes*, Book 10) wrote about it directly: right as you sit down to do the holy thing, a flood of reasons to quit appears, dressed up to feel like *your own* thoughts. And there is a well-attested principle in Orthodox ascetic teaching: **the greater the good you are about to do, the harder the enemy fights you right before it.** So the pull to flee was not failure — it was confirmation. **Staying in the seat was itself repentance.** The way to fight it: name it plainly in the heart — *"This is not from God. I'm staying"* — and stand. *"Resist the devil, and he will flee from you"* (James 4:7, NKJV).

**3. How to wait — and how to fight the flooding thoughts in prayer.** King prayed the **Jesus Prayer** and the thoughts still flooded. This is the key thing to understand: **a flooding mind is the normal experience of everyone who prays seriously, including the saints.** You cannot stop the thoughts from coming; what you *can* do is **not chase them.** The whole prayer is in the **returning.** Every time a thought floods in and you gently come back to *"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner,"* **that return is the victory — not the silence.** Like a rock that does not try to stop the waves but simply stays. And when even the prayer feels impossible, one line is enough: *"Lord, I believe; help my unbelief"* (Mark 9:24, NKJV). King's own instinct — *"I'm just going to stand with Jesus"* — is exactly right: you do not have to win the argument with every thought; just keep standing next to Him and let Him fight. *"The Lord will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace"* (Exodus 14:14, NKJV).

**4. Discerning the sleepiness — the master-skill of the spiritual life.** King asked: is the heavy sleepiness the enemy, or God giving rest, or just tiredness? The Church's answer is not "what is this feeling?" but **"where does it lead?"** This is **discernment**, and the Lord gave the test: *"By their fruits you will know them"* (Matthew 7:16, NKJV). Apply it:
- **If the drowsiness pulls you *away***, out of prayer, toward giving up, **that is the enemy.** The desert Fathers specifically warned that acedia attacks with heaviness and sleep during prayer and vigil, to drag you out. Resist that kind: stand, move, splash the Jesus Prayer over it.
- **If it is the rest that comes *after* you have poured your heart out, and you wake in *peace*, closer to God — that is God's gift.** King already has the evidence: once before, he cried, prayed, slept in church, and **woke in peace.** **Peace is the fingerprint of God** — the enemy never leaves you peaceful, he leaves you anxious, ashamed, or empty. *"He gives His beloved sleep"* (Psalm 126:2 OSB / 127:2 NKJV). *"Come to Me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest"* (Matthew 11:28, NKJV).
- **And sometimes tired is just tired**, and that is not "un-spiritual." King had not eaten and had been at war for a while. God made the body; needing rest is not a failure. The picture is **Elijah**: broken and wanting to die, he collapsed, and God did not scold him, God let him sleep, then sent an angel to wake him gently: *"Arise and eat,"* for the journey was too great for him (3 Kingdoms 19 / 1 Kings 19). Then came the still, small voice. The wise move in the chair: stay awake enough not to miss the priest, confess, **then go eat and rest**, like Elijah, strengthened for the road.

**5. The urge to church and the tears — and what *theosis* actually means.** King wondered if the recurring urge to go to church, and the deep cry in his brokenness, is "theosis." A gentle correction so he has the word right: **theosis** (deification) means *becoming united with God* — so filled with His grace that you become, as Scripture says, a **"partaker of the divine nature"** (2 Peter 1:4, NKJV). It is the **goal** of the whole Christian life. St Athanasius put it famously (in sense): *"God became man so that man might become god"* — that is, share God's life by grace, never becoming God by nature. So what King feels is not theosis itself, but **the grace that pulls him toward it**, and it has two precious names in the Fathers:
- **The urge to church when life crushes him**, that is the Holy Spirit *drawing* him; it comes *to* him, he does not manufacture it. God seeking him out.
- **The crying out in brokenness** (*"only God can save me"*) — this is **compunction** (Greek *penthos*) and **godly sorrow**, and far from weakness it is one of the *highest* gifts. St John Climacus (*The Ladder of Divine Ascent*, Step 7, "joy-making mourning") and St Isaac the Syrian treat the **gift of tears** as a sign that grace is actively at work. *"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and a contrite heart… You will not despise"* (Psalm 50:17 OSB / 51:17 NKJV). *"Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation"* (2 Corinthians 7:10, NKJV). Those shattered, weeping nights are not King falling apart — they are God breaking hard ground to plant something.

**6. "It felt easy at the start, now it's harder", the normal pattern of first grace.** King noticed faith felt sweet and easy when he first became Orthodox in October, and now that he is "locked in" it feels harder. This is **completely normal and is maturing, not backsliding.** The Fathers teach that God gives **beginners a taste of sweetness**, a first grace, freely given, to draw them in and show them it is real. Then, as a soul goes deeper, that *felt* sweetness often pulls back, not because God left, but to grow a child who needs the sweet into a son who **stays even when it is dry.** Faith that only works when it feels good is not yet strong; faith that stays when it is dry is becoming real. Every saint walked this road.

**7. Being received by Chrismation (King's situation, learned today).** King was **baptised in the Roman Catholic Church** — water, in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. The Orthodox Church takes that seriously; we confess *"one baptism for the remission of sins"* (the Creed; Ephesians 4:5, *"one Lord, one faith, one baptism"*). **How a person baptised as a Catholic is received into Orthodoxy is decided by the bishop and the priest, and it varies** — some are received by **Chrismation** (the prior baptism accepted, by *economia*), some in stricter practice by **Baptism**. *This is not a thing a study aid can settle — it belongs to Fr Bogdan and his bishop.* In King's case, **Fr Bogdan said he can be received by Chrismation.** What that means:
- **Chrismation** is being **sealed with the Holy Spirit.** The priest anoints with **Holy Chrism** (fragrant oil consecrated by the bishops) on the forehead, eyes, nostrils, lips, ears, chest, hands, and feet, saying each time: **"The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen."** *"He who… has anointed us is God, who also has sealed us and given us the Spirit in our hearts as a guarantee"* (2 Corinthians 1:21-22, NKJV). It is a personal Pentecost.
- It **completes** his Catholic baptism and brings him into the **fullness** of the Orthodox Church, he is not told his baptism "was nothing."
- After Chrismation he may **receive Holy Communion.** The Chalice King held back from out of right humility *opens* to him, in the proper order he himself rightly sensed.

This is why the enemy fought so hard at the door: not over a quiet half-hour, but over a son being **sealed with the Holy Spirit and brought home to the Chalice.**

**8. Secret sins, "unavoidable" sins, and the "lesser evil" trap (King's deeper question).** King realised there are sins he is *not even aware of*, that some sins feel *unavoidable*, and that sometimes life seems a *choice of the lesser evil*, and that all of this could fold back into the fear of "using God." Three faithful answers:
- **You do not have to dig out and list every hidden sin.** The Orthodox prayers ask forgiveness for sins *"voluntary and involuntary, known and unknown, in word, deed, and thought"* — the unknown ones are **included on purpose**. *"Who can understand his errors? Cleanse me from my secret faults"* (Psalm 18:12-13 OSB / 19:12); *"Search me, O God, and know my heart… and see if there is any wicked way in me"* (Psalm 138:23-24 OSB / 139). God reveals the heart **progressively**, a lifetime long, as much as you can bear — and the Fathers noticed that **the closer you get to the Light, the more dust you see**, which is a sign of *growth*, not decline (it is why the saints called themselves the worst of sinners). The tax collector who simply cried *"God, be merciful to me, a sinner"* — listing nothing — went home justified (Luke 18:13). Mercy covers what you cannot even name.
- **"Unavoidable" is half-true, and the half matters.** Yes, we all stumble and the Church is never surprised by it (*"there is no man who lives and does not sin"*; 1 John 1:8; Ecclesiastes 7:20). **But God never traps you** — He does not command the impossible and then condemn: *"God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape"* (1 Corinthians 10:13, NKJV). So "unavoidable" honestly describes the **fallen world**, not a locked door with no way out. Hold the humility ("I will stumble") without letting it become permission ("so why fight").
- **The "lesser of two evils", handle with care, and never self-judge it.** Genuine moral dilemmas exist, and the Church does speak of choosing the path of **least harm with a repentant heart** (and of *economia*). But the mind loves to **pre-label** something "the lesser evil" so it does not have to fight it, that is the loophole wearing a serious face. The honest move is never to be your own judge here: **bring the actual situation to Fr Bogdan**, name it plainly, and let him weigh it.
- **How it all folds into the "using God" fear:** every one of these can be held **two ways**, and the tell is always the same, *cold loophole* ("it's unavoidable / the lesser evil, so grace covers it") vs *honest and grieved* ("I keep stumbling, I'm not pretending it's fine, I bring it to the light again"). **The very fact King fears he might be using God as a loophole is proof he is not**, a man gaming the system does not lie awake afraid he is gaming the system. The single posture that answers all of it: **bring it into the light, do not pre-justify it, and keep getting up.** And do not let "I haven't figured it all out yet" become one more reason to delay, **Confession itself is where this gets worked out, over a lifetime, not solved in one anxious sitting.**

## How to behave in Confession (a first confession)
1. **Tell the priest it is your first confession.** He will slow down and guide you; you do not need the "right words."
2. **Picture Christ, not the priest.** It will likely be at an icon, the Gospel book, or a cross. The priest is the witness; Christ is the healer.
3. **Be honest and specific, but plain.** Name the sins simply, *"I have done this; I struggle with this"*, without dressing them up, blaming circumstances, or minimising. Naming a thing in the light strips its power.
4. **Do not perform and do not despair.** You are not there to impress or to grovel, only to be truthful. He has heard everything before; nothing will shock him or lower you in his eyes.
5. **Listen to his counsel.** He may give a word of guidance, or a small *epitimia* (penance), not punishment, but **medicine** for the soul. Receive it.
6. **Receive the absolution.** He may lay his stole (*epitrachil*) over your head and say the prayer of forgiveness. That moment is **Christ Himself** forgiving you. Let it land, you will walk out lighter than you came in.

## The seven lenses (the deep read)
- **Theology:** Confession is the Mystery by which the baptismal grace, dimmed by sin, is restored — "a second baptism" of **tears** (the Fathers' phrase). Its power is Christ's own, delegated to the Church (John 20:22-23); the priest is the icon of the Physician, not a substitute for Him.
- **The Why:** sin is *distance from the God you love*, not mere rule-breaking (see the "Sin, the Passions & Repentance" lesson). So forgiveness is not a legal acquittal but a **reunion** — the Father running to the returning son (Luke 15:20). That is why Confession heals rather than merely pardons.
- **Lived Tradition:** the rhythm is **examine → confess → receive absolution → commune.** Many Orthodox confess *before* the Liturgy precisely to approach the Chalice with a clean heart. The *pace* of Confession and Communion is **Fr Bogdan's** to set, not the study aid's.
- **Connections:** ties straight to "Humility, the Accuser & Discerning Your Thoughts" (godly sorrow vs despairing shame), to "Slave to Sin, Free in Christ" (Romans 6, 8, the war within), to "Sin, the Passions & Repentance" (the very matter King brings to Confession), and to the Divine Liturgy lessons (Confession prepares for the Chalice).
