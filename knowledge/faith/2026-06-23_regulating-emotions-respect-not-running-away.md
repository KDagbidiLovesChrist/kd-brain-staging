# Regulating Emotions, Respect, and Not Running Away
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** 🕊️ Lived Tradition / Formation (the heart) · *born from a real family storm and his sister Cleo's God-given advice*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
This lesson came out of a hard real night, not a book. After the credit union only part-approved King's
loan, his dad kept pushing to know about it; King said "I don't want to talk about it" and warned he would
block him, then blocked him, and it spilled into the family group chat and a clash with his sister Cleo.
When the heat cooled, Cleo gave him advice that was clearly from God, the same thing his study had been
teaching: running away (cutting people off, fleeing) does not actually work, because some people will still
say what they want to say. So the skill to learn is to **regulate your emotions**: when something triggers
you, pause, let the person say their piece, take it in, and move on, instead of reacting or running.
Importantly, the boundary itself was fine, it was the **tone** and the **threat to block** that made it
land as disrespect, and in their African culture (and in Scripture) respect for parents is a big thing.
AND, held alongside that: if King genuinely is not ready to talk about something yet, he does not have to,
that is a real and fair boundary, he just delivers it calmly and respectfully. This matters for King
because it turns a painful night into growth: he is learning to stay, hear people out, keep his peace
without cutting people off, and honour his family while still having boundaries.

## In one line
Running away and cutting people off does not work; the skill is to regulate your emotions (pause, hear
them out, let them say their piece, move on), hold boundaries with respect not threats, honour your
family, and step back only to return, not to flee, while it is still fine to defer a talk you are genuinely
not ready for.

## Where this came from
King's real evening of 2026-06-23: the loan was only part-approved, his dad pressed to know about it, King
set a boundary and then blocked him, the family group chat flared, he clashed with and briefly blocked his
sister Cleo. On reconciling, Cleo gave him wise, loving advice (which matched his faith study), and King

boundaries respectfully.

## The lesson

**1. The boundary was right; the tone is the growth edge.** Wanting privacy and peace is fair (*"a time to
keep silence,"* Ecclesiastes 3:7; *"keep your heart with all diligence,"* Proverbs 4:23). What slipped was
the delivery, the threat to block. *"A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger"*
(Proverbs 15:1). Same boundary, soft tone, no threat.

**2. Regulate your emotions when triggered.** *"Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to
wrath; for the wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God"* (James 1:19-20). Patience and
self-control are fruit of the Holy Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23), so this is God growing something in King,
not just self-help. The tools: name it ("I am triggered right now"), take a breath, and pray the **Jesus
Prayer** silently (*"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me"*), which buys space and invites grace
at the same time.

**3. Hearing people out is not agreeing, and not losing your boundary.** Some people will say what they
want to say regardless. Let them say it, take it in, and move on. You cannot control them, only your
response (*"as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all,"* Romans 12:18). You can fully hear your
dad out and still calmly say "I would rather not discuss it." Listening is not obeying.

**4. Honour your family (culture and Scripture agree).** In African culture respect for parents and elders
runs deep, and Scripture says the same: *"Honour your father and mother"* (Ephesians 6:2). Respect and
boundaries are not enemies, you can have a limit and still carry it with honour. And do not weaponise the
faith against family or judge their Christianity, mind your own plank first (*"why do you look at the speck
in your brother's eye, but do not consider the plank in your own eye?"* Matthew 7:3-5).

**5. Do not always run away, but healthy space is allowed.** The difference is the **return**. Healthy:
step back to cool down, then come back to face and repair it. Running away: leaving to avoid it forever,
cutting people off. Even Jesus withdrew to pray and rest (Luke 5:16), but He never fled the hard thing, He
stayed, all the way to the Cross, and love *"bears all things, endures all things"* (1 Corinthians 13:7).
King is already practising the healthy version: he took space, but he is going back to unblock and mend.

**6. But it is still fine to defer a talk you are not ready for.** Held alongside all the above: if King
genuinely is not ready to talk about something now, he does not have to. That is a real, legitimate
boundary. He just delivers it calmly and respectfully, "I am not ready to talk about this right now," and
then disengages gently, no threat, door open.

**7. This is godly sorrow, not condemnation.** King saw his fault and it grieved him, and that grief is
producing change, which is exactly right: *"godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation... but
the sorrow of the world produces death"* (2 Corinthians 7:10). He must not let it curdle into "I am a bad
person who makes everyone walk on eggshells," that is the accuser. *"There is no condemnation to those who
are in Christ Jesus"* (Romans 8:1). Cleo's hard words were a gift: *"faithful are the wounds of a friend"*
(Proverbs 27:6).

## Live it this week
When something triggers you: pause, name it, breathe, and silently pray the Jesus Prayer before you speak.
Let the person finish and say their piece, take it in, then respond calmly or simply move on. Hold any
boundary with a soft tone and no threats. If you step back, commit to coming back to mend it. (King's plan:
unblock Dad and Cleo tomorrow, apologise for the way he did it, and use mute, not block, next time.)
