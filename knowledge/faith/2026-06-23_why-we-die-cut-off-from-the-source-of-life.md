# Why We Die: Death as Being Cut Off from God, the Source of Life
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** 🧠 Theology (the Fall + human nature) · *born from King's question*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King asked: why exactly did humans have to die after disconnecting from God? The Orthodox answer is gentle and makes deep sense. God is not just *a* living thing, God IS Life itself, the source of all existence. We are made out of nothing, so we do not own life in ourselves; we only have it as a gift, by staying joined to God who is Life. While the first humans stayed united to God, they shared His life and would not have rotted away or died. Sin is turning away from God. And to cut yourself off from the only Source of life is, by the very nature of the thing, to start falling toward death, the way a lamp unplugged from the power goes dark, or a branch cut from the tree withers, or a fish out of water dies. So death is not mainly God angrily killing us for breaking a rule; it is the natural result of letting go of Life Himself. The Bible even says plainly that God did not make death and takes no pleasure in it; death came in through sin and the envy of the devil. And here is the surprising mercy: God let physical death exist so that we would not live forever stuck in a broken, sinful state; death became a temporary limit on evil, and the very door Christ would later walk through to destroy death from inside. This matters for King because it shows God as the giver of life, never the lover of death, and it makes the whole rescue in Christ make sense.

## In one line
We die because sin cut us off from God who IS Life; death is the natural fruit of separation from the Source of life (not mainly an angry punishment), God did not make or desire death, and He even turned physical death into a mercy and a doorway.

## The teaching

**1. God alone is Life; we hold life only by communion with Him.** God is being and life itself. Creatures are made *from nothing* and do not carry life in themselves; we live by staying connected to the One who is Life. St Athanasius (*On the Incarnation*) teaches that humanity was given the grace to remain incorruptible by staying united to God through the image, knowing and loving Him. Life was always a gift held in relationship, not a possession we own.

**2. Sin is unplugging from the Source, and death is what follows.** God warned: *"in the day that you eat from it you shall die by death"* (Genesis 2:17, the Septuagint/OSB reading). This is not only a sentence; it is a description of cause and effect. To turn from God who is Life is to fall toward death and corruption, the way a branch cut from the vine withers, or a lamp unplugged goes dark. St Athanasius: having turned from God, man began to slide back toward the nothing he was made from. *"Through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin"* (Romans 5:12, NKJV); *"the wages of sin is death"* (Romans 6:23, NKJV).

**3. Two deaths, not one.** Spiritual death (the soul cut off from God) is the root, and it happens the moment we turn away. Physical death (the soul separating from the body) follows from it. The fall brought both. The deepest death is the spiritual one; physical death is its visible shadow.

**4. God did NOT make death and does not delight in it.** This is vital, so you never picture God as a lover of death. *"God did not make death, nor does He delight in the destruction of the living"* (Wisdom of Solomon 1:13, in the Orthodox Old Testament). *"God created man for incorruption... but through the envy of the devil, death entered the world"* (Wisdom 2:23-24). Death is an intruder, an enemy, not a thing God wanted for us.

**5. The surprising mercy of physical death.** After the fall God barred the way to the tree of life *"lest he reach out his hand... and eat, and live forever"* (Genesis 3:22). The Fathers read this as mercy: God did not want man immortalized *in* his fallen, sinful state. So physical death became a temporary limit on evil. St Gregory the Theologian (Oration 45, On Pascha) teaches that death was set as a limit so that evil would not be immortal, and so "the punishment is changed into a mercy." Death thus becomes the very thing Christ will later use, entering it to destroy it (see the companion lesson on Hades).

## Live it this week
When you feel the pull toward sin, remember what sin really is: not just breaking a rule, but unplugging from Life. Reach back to the Source with one line, *"Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me,"* and stay plugged in.

## Prayer
