# The Defense · Heresies by Council (the Seven Ecumenical Councils)

> *A scaffold of the **Seven Ecumenical Councils**, the Church's "high court," where the whole Church,
> gathered, defended the faith against a specific error. In Orthodoxy we mostly **meet heresy through its
> cure**: you learn the crooked by knowing the straight. This file states **only the well-established
> headline** for each council (the heresy, the truth it defended, the core decision). Finer detail, the
> Fathers who led, and any quotes are marked **🔜 to study** rather than written from memory, they fill in
> as the study's **Church-history spine reaches each council**. Never fabricate; **big questions → Fr Bogdan.***

> *"Be ready always to give an answer… **with meekness and fear**." — 1 Peter 3:15*

**How to use this:** the headline of each council is enough to *recognise* the error and name the truth.
When ② The Tradition / the history spine reaches a council, that council gets a full verified lesson
(book-intro style: who, when, where, the Fathers, the canons, the wording) and its **🔜 to study** notes

## 1. Nicaea I · AD 325
- **Heresy answered:** **Arianism**, the teaching (Arius) that the Son was a created being, the highest
  creature but not truly God ("there was a time when He was not").
- **Truth defended:** the Son is truly God, **of one essence (homoousios)** with the Father, not made,
  but begotten; fully God, eternally.
- **Core decision:** the original **Nicene Creed** (the first part of the Creed we still say), confessing
  the Son *"of one essence with the Father."*
- 🔜 *To study as the history spine reaches it:* St Athanasius' role; St Nicholas; the exact Greek
  (homoousios vs homoiousios); the dating of Pascha; the canons.

## 2. Constantinople I · AD 381
- **Heresy answered:** chiefly the **Macedonians / Pneumatomachi** ("Spirit-fighters"), who denied the
  full divinity of the **Holy Spirit**; also the lingering Arian dispute.
- **Truth defended:** the **Holy Spirit is truly God**, worshipped and glorified together with the Father
  and the Son, completing the confession of the Holy Trinity.
- **Core decision:** **completed the Nicene Creed** into the form the Church prays today (the
  Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed), expanding the article on the Holy Spirit.
- 🔜 *To study:* the Cappadocian Fathers (St Basil the Great, St Gregory the Theologian, St Gregory of
  Nyssa); how the Creed's wording on the Spirit was settled.

## 3. Ephesus · AD 431
- **Heresy answered:** **Nestorianism**, which so divided Christ into two that it effectively made Him two
  persons, and refused to call Mary **Theotokos** ("God-bearer"), preferring only "Christ-bearer."
- **Truth defended:** Christ is **one Person**, and Mary is rightly called **Theotokos**, because the one
  she bore *is* God the Word made flesh. (The title guards *Christ*: it's a statement about who Jesus is.)
- **Core decision:** affirmed Mary as **Theotokos** and the unity of Christ's Person.
- 🔜 *To study:* St Cyril of Alexandria's role; the precise Christological wording; the link to the icons
  and to "Why honour Mary?" *(note: a full verified Theotokos lesson is still **🔜 to build**, see
  `STRUCTURE.md`).*

## 4. Chalcedon · AD 451
- **Heresy answered:** **Monophysitism / Eutychianism**, that in Christ the human nature was swallowed up
  by the divine, leaving only **one nature**.
- **Truth defended:** Christ is **one Person in two natures**, fully God and fully man, *"without
  confusion, without change, without division, without separation."*
- **Core decision:** the **Chalcedonian Definition** (the "two natures in one Person" formula).
- 🔜 *To study:* the Tome of St Leo; the four adverbs; the historical split with the
  Oriental Orthodox (a fair, careful account, **→ Fr Bogdan** for this one).

## 5. Constantinople II · AD 553
- **Heresy answered:** lingering disputes after Chalcedon (commonly summarised as the **"Three Chapters"**
  controversy), defending the Chalcedonian faith against both Nestorian-leaning and Monophysite-leaning
  readings.
- **Truth defended:** **upheld and clarified Chalcedon**, that the one Christ is truly one Person in two
  natures, read rightly.
- **Core decision:** confirmed and interpreted the earlier councils' Christology.
- 🔜 *To study:* the detail of the Three Chapters; the relation to St Cyril's theology; (this council's
  specifics are technical, **🔜 to study carefully** when the spine reaches it, and **→ Fr Bogdan**).

## 6. Constantinople III · AD 680-681
- **Heresy answered:** **Monothelitism**, that Christ had only **one will** (the divine), not a human will.
- **Truth defended:** Christ has **two wills** (divine and human) and two energies, in harmony, because a
  full human nature includes a human will (so He could freely say *"not My will, but Yours, be done"* as
  truly man). A real man with no human will would not be a real man.
- **Core decision:** confirmed **two wills in Christ**.
- 🔜 *To study:* St Maximus the Confessor's witness and suffering; how the human will freely follows the
  divine; Gethsemane read through this council.

## 7. Nicaea II · AD 787
- **Heresy answered:** **Iconoclasm**, the rejection and destruction of holy icons as supposed idolatry.
- **Truth defended:** **icons are right and good**, because **God became visible in Christ**, He can be
  depicted; and the honour shown to an icon **passes to the prototype** (St Basil), so veneration is not
  idolatry. Worship (*latria*) is for God alone; icons receive honour/veneration (*proskynesis*).
- **Core decision:** **restored the veneration of icons**; distinguished worship (God alone) from the
  honour given to icons.
- **✅ Covered in a full verified lesson:** `../2026-06-28_icons-and-veneration.md` (window not idol;
  veneration vs worship; the honour passes to the prototype; the Incarnation as the key; St John of Damascus;
  the Ark→Theotokos type; honouring the saints & the Theotokos), plus `../2026-06-27_the-practices-that-form-us.md`
  (icons section) and the Quick Answer "Why venerate icons?" in `QUICK_ANSWERS.md`.
- ✅ **St John of Damascus** now cited ("I do not worship matter; I worship the God of matter, who became
  matter for my sake"); the ***latreia* (worship, God alone) vs *proskynesis* (veneration/honour)** distinction
  is taught.
- 🔜 *Still to study:* the two phases of iconoclasm and the "Triumph of Orthodoxy" (843); St Theodore the Studite.
