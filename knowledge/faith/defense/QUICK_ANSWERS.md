## 1. "How do you even know God is real?"
*(from `../2026-06-21_how-do-we-know-god-is-real.md`)*

- **The short answer:** We don't mostly *prove* God like a sum (1+1=2). We come to *know* Him like a
  **Person**, by signposts that point to Him, by what He did in Jesus, and most of all by **meeting** Him.
- **Scripture:** *"Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good"* (Psalm 34:8). And *"if Christ is not risen…
  you are still in your sins!"* (1 Corinthians 15:17) — the whole faith rests on a real event in history.
- **Jesus:** When Nathanael scoffed, the answer wasn't a debate, it was two words: *"Come and see"*
  (John 1:46). You believe after you meet Him, not before.
- **Father:** St Gregory Palamas, like the sun, you can't grab its burning core, but its light and warmth
  truly reach you and *are* the sun. God's deepest self stays beyond us; His grace really touches us
  (essence/energies). And St Seraphim of Sarov: the whole Christian life is *"the acquisition of the Holy
  Spirit"*, the proof shows up in changed people.
- **The honest part (don't dodge):** no single proof forces everyone, and that's not God failing, *love
  can't be forced.* So He stays findable but not undeniable, to keep us free. Doubt can live inside faith:
  *"Lord, I believe; help my unbelief"* (Mark 9:24).
- **How we live it:** each morning pray one honest line, *"Lord, if You are there, show me; I want to
  know You"*, then go to the Liturgy with eyes open. Knowing God grows by getting *near*, not by arguing
  from far off.

## 2. "Why the Church / Tradition · why not just the Bible?"
*(from `../2026-06-21_whole-arsenal-tradition-apologetics.md`)*

- **The short answer:** The Bible doesn't float free of the Church, it lives *within* the Church's whole
  life and memory (**Holy Tradition**), with Scripture as its God-breathed heart. It's not "Bible vs
  Tradition", the Bible is Tradition's centre.
- **Scripture:** *"hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle"*
  (2 Thessalonians 2:15). And *"earnestly contend for the faith which was once delivered unto the saints"*
  (Jude 1:3) — our job is to guard and pass on the deposit, not reinvent it.
- **Jesus:** the risen Lord, *"beginning at Moses and all the prophets, expounded… the things concerning
  himself"* (Luke 24:27) — He read all of Scripture as pointing to Himself, inside the community He founded.
- **Church / Council:** the measure is the **consensus of the Fathers** + the Ecumenical Councils, what
  was believed *"everywhere, always, by all"* (St Vincent of Lérins, *Commonitorium*). A lone writer or a
  local synod can err; what the whole Church receives stands.
- **Father / witness:** the **Didache** (the earliest post-apostolic manual, ~AD 50, 120) already shows
  Triune baptism, the Eucharist, and fasting, proof there's no gap between the apostles and the Church's
  practice; the faith flowed straight on.
- **How we live it:** when you read or watch anything about the faith, ask, *does it agree with the
  Church's settled teaching? Is it from a trustworthy well? Should I run it past Fr Bogdan?* That turns the
  whole internet into a safe library.

## 3. "Isn't the Cross God punishing Jesus to satisfy His wrath?"
*(from `../2026-06-23_the-cross-christus-victor-not-an-angry-god.md`)*

- **The short answer:** God truly *did* send His Son, and Christ truly died for us, we say it in the
  Creed every Liturgy. The Orthodox difference is about the *meaning*: the Cross is **God's love conquering
  death and healing us from the inside** (a rescue and a hospital), **not** the Father pouring out wrath on
  the Son to pay a legal debt. **Jesus does not save us *from an angry Father***, Father, Son and Spirit
  are one God in love, acting together.
- **State the other view fairly first:** the Western "courtroom" picture (Anselm's satisfaction; the
  Reformed penal substitution) sees sin as a debt or an offense to God's justice/wrath that had to be
  satisfied, with the Father laying our punishment on Jesus. There is real love and real truth in it, and
  thoughtful Catholics and Protestants insist the Father is *not* truly against the Son.
- **Scripture:** *"God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself"* (2 Corinthians 5:19) — God *in*
  Christ, not God *against* Christ. And the rescue: *"…that through death He might destroy him who had the
  power of death, that is, the devil"* (Hebrews 2:14–15).
- **Jesus / the Father's heart:** *"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son"*
  (John 3:16); *"while we were still sinners, Christ died for us"* (Romans 5:8) — love came first, not a
  bill to be paid.
- **Father:** St Gregory the Theologian (*Oration 45, On Pascha*) asks to *whom* the ransom was paid and
  refuses to say the Father demanded the blood of His Son, he will not let the Cross become an angry God
  needing to be paid.
- **Keep the strong words too (honesty):** Orthodoxy fully keeps the Bible's language, the Lamb of God, a
  real sacrifice, a ransom for many, *"He bore our sins"* (Isaiah 53). The difference is what they point
  at: love conquering death, not wrath being vented.
- **How we live it:** look at a crucifix and say, not *"see how angry God was,"* but *"see how far God's
  love went to rescue me."* Let the Cross read as love, not fear.

## 4. "Why venerate icons · isn't that idolatry?"
*(from `../2026-06-27_the-practices-that-form-us.md`, icons section)*

- **The short answer:** We do **not** worship icons, worship belongs to God alone. We **venerate** them
  (honour, a bow, a kiss), the way you'd kiss a photo of someone you love. The honour isn't trapped in the
  wood and paint; it passes *through* the image to the real person.
- **Father:** St Basil the Great, *"the honour shown to the image passes to the prototype."* The honour
  goes *through* the icon to the one pictured.
- **Church / Council:** images are possible because **God became visible in Christ**, the invisible God
  took a real, picturable human face. This was defended and confirmed at the **7th Ecumenical Council
  (Nicaea II, 787)** against the iconoclasts.
- **Why it's not idolatry:** an idol is a false god you bow *to as god*; an icon is a window you look
  *through* to the true God and His saints. The line is worship (God alone) vs veneration (honour shown to
  the holy).
- **How we live it:** set up a small **prayer corner** at home, an icon and a candle on a shelf, facing
  east if you can, and stand there for morning and evening prayer. Give your body a *place* that means
  "now I'm with God."
