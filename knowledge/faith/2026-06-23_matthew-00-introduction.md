## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
Before we read a book of the Bible, it helps to first know who wrote it and why. This lesson is about Matthew, the man who wrote the very first Gospel (a book telling the story of Jesus' life). Matthew used to be a tax collector, a man who took money for the Romans, so his own people hated him as a traitor. But Jesus walked up to him and said just two words, "Follow Me," and Matthew got up and went, leaving his old life behind. He later became one of the Twelve Apostles (Jesus' twelve closest friends, sent out to spread the faith). He wrote his Gospel mostly for Jewish Christians to prove one big thing: that Jesus is the promised King and Saviour the Old Testament had been waiting for all along. Why this matters for you: a man everyone wrote off as a sinner was called by Christ and made an apostle, and that same Jesus is calling you out of whatever holds you back.

## In one line
Matthew, a despised tax collector whom Christ called with two words, wrote the first Gospel for Jewish Christians to prove one thing: **Jesus is the promised Messiah and King, the fulfilment of everything the Old Testament was waiting for.**

## 1. Who was Matthew?
- He was a **tax collector** ("publican") sitting at the tax booth in Capernaum — also called **Levi** (Mark 2:14; Luke 5:27). Tax collectors worked for Rome and were despised as traitors and sinners by their own people.
- Christ called him with two words: *"As Jesus passed on from there, He saw a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office. And He said to him, 'Follow Me.' So he arose and followed Him"* (Matthew 9:9, NKJV). He left it all at once.
- At the meal in his house, when the Pharisees objected to Jesus eating with tax collectors and sinners, the Lord said: *"I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance"* (Matthew 9:13, NKJV). **Matthew is living proof of that mercy** — the tax collector became an evangelist.
- He became one of the **Twelve Apostles** (*"Matthew the tax collector,"* Matthew 10:3) — he keeps that humble title on himself. Tradition holds he preached widely and died a martyr; the Church commemorates him on **November 16**.

## 2. The Gospel of Matthew (the OSB book intro)
- **Author:** the Apostle Matthew, the earliest Church witness is unanimous (Papias, St Irenaeus).
- **Written for:** especially **Jewish Christians**, which is why it overflows with the Old Testament, repeating *"that it might be fulfilled"* to show Jesus completing the prophets.
- **The big theme:** Jesus is the **promised Messiah and King**, *"the son of David, the son of Abraham"* (1:1), and the **Kingdom of Heaven** has come. (Matthew often says "Kingdom of Heaven" out of Jewish reverence for the divine Name.)
- **The shape, five great discourses.** Matthew arranges Jesus' teaching into **five** big blocks (echoing the **five books of Moses**, presenting Christ as the new Moses, the new Law-giver), framed by His birth and His Passion, Resurrection:
  1. The **Sermon on the Mount** (chs. 5, 7), the life of the Kingdom.
  2. The **Mission** of the disciples (ch. 10).
  3. The **Parables** of the Kingdom (ch. 13).
  4. **Life in the Church / community** (ch. 18).
  5. The **Last Things** (chs. 24, 25).
- **His symbol:** in the Church's traditional symbolism of the four evangelists (the four living creatures), Matthew is **the man / the winged man (angel)**, because his Gospel opens with the human genealogy, the *humanity* of Christ. (Mark = lion, Luke = ox, John = eagle.)

## Why this matters before we read
The OSB (Fr Bogdan's plan) teaches you to **know the book before you read it**, who wrote it, to whom, and why, so you read with the author's purpose in view. Knowing Matthew writes to show *Jesus fulfils the Old Testament* is the key that unlocks all those "that it might be fulfilled" lines we already met in chapters 1, 2.

## The backing (verified)
- Matthew 9:9, 9:13, 10:3 (NKJV) · Mark 2:14 · Luke 5:27 (Matthew = Levi the tax collector).
- Matthew 1:1 — "son of David, the son of Abraham."
- The five-discourse structure and the man/angel symbol are the standard, ancient reading of the Church (and the OSB's own introduction); read the OSB's "Introduction to Matthew" in your own Bible for its exact wording and notes.

## Live it this week
Take heart from *who wrote this Gospel*: a man everyone wrote off as a sinner, called by Christ and made an apostle. *"I came to call sinners."* That includes you, in your struggle, you are exactly the kind of person Jesus calls. When you open Matthew, read it knowing the writer was rescued the same way you are being rescued.
