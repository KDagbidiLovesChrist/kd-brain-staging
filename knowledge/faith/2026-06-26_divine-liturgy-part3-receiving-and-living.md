# How the Divine Liturgy Works · Part 3: Receiving Communion + the Liturgy After the Liturgy

*Taught with the seven lenses, beauty + formation foregrounded, baby-simple + story-flow.*

> *"With the fear of God, with faith and love, draw near.", the deacon's call before Communion*

---

## Where we are
Part 1 = what the Liturgy *is* and *why* ("the work of the people," heaven on earth, the Eucharist at its
heart). Part 2 = walking *through* it, the Entrances, the Cherubic Hymn, the Anaphora, the Sanctus, the
**epiclesis** (the priest calling the Holy Spirit down on the gifts), and Communion. Now Part 3 is the most
personal part: **how do I get ready to actually receive Communion**, and **what do I do when the Liturgy
ends?** Because the Liturgy isn't meant to stop at the church door; it's meant to become your whole week.

## 1) Getting ready to receive · the heart first
Communion isn't a snack and it isn't a reward for the perfect. It's **the Body and Blood of Christ**, God
giving you *Himself*, so we come **prepared, humble, and reverent**, never casual. St Paul warned the
early Christians not to receive *"in an unworthy manner"* without "discerning the Lord's body" (1
Corinthians 11:27–29). That's not meant to scare you away — it's meant to wake you up to **Who** you're
receiving.

How the Church helps us prepare (your parish + **Fr Bogdan** set the exact practice, this is the shape,
not a rulebook from me):
- **The heart:** come with **repentance** and **forgiveness**, make peace with people first (*"first be
  reconciled to your brother,"* Matthew 5:24). Pride and grudges are the real barrier, not imperfection.
- **Prayer:** the Church has **pre-Communion prayers** (a short set you pray beforehand) that put the heart
  in the right place: *"I believe, O Lord, and I confess that You are truly the Christ… receive me today as
  a partaker of Your Mystical Supper."*
- **Fasting:** we usually come to Communion **fasting** (an empty stomach that morning, and keeping the
  Church's fast days), the **body** joining the **soul** in saying "God first." Small and faithful, the
  way Fr Bogdan guides you.
- **Confession:** the Church's medicine for a heavy conscience. In the Orthodox tradition, **Confession**
  (saying your sins to God before the priest, and receiving absolution) clears the way to the chalice.
  *This is pastoral, it's Fr Bogdan's to guide, not mine.* (This connects straight to your earlier
  lessons, "slave to sin, free in Christ" and the accuser: Confession is exactly where you take that war.)

## 2) Receiving · "with the fear of God, with faith and love"
When the gifts are brought out, the deacon (or priest) calls: ***"With the fear of God, with faith and
love, draw near."*** Each word is a key:
- **Fear of God**, not terror, but **awe**: knowing this is truly God, not bread.
- **Faith**, trusting it really is His Body and Blood.
- **Love**, coming *because* you love Him and want to be one with Him.

We approach slowly, hands crossed over the chest, and receive the Body and Blood together on a spoon from
the **chalice** (the cup), the priest saying your (baptismal) name. It is **union**, Christ entering you,
to heal and fill you with His life (**theosis** again, the whole point of everything). Afterward the
Church gives thanks with **post-Communion prayers**: *"Glory to You, glory to You, glory to You, O God."*

## 3) The dismissal · and the "liturgy after the Liturgy"
At the end the priest says, *"Let us depart in peace,"* and blesses everyone, and we're **sent out**. Here's
the beautiful, demanding truth the Fathers teach: **the Liturgy doesn't end, it changes location.** What
you received at the altar, you now carry into the street. There's an old Orthodox phrase for this: **"the
liturgy after the Liturgy."**
- You've just received Christ. Now go and **be Christ's hands**, to your family, your workmates at the
  data centre, the stranger, *"the least of these"* (Matthew 25:40, where the King says whatever you did
  for the lowest, you did for Him).
- The peace you were given, you **give away**. The mercy you received, you **show**.
- This is where the recap meets real life: *regulating your emotions* with your dad and Cleo, *bearing
  fruit worthy of repentance*, *witnessing in peace not debate*, that **is** the liturgy after the
  Liturgy. Sunday isn't separate from Monday; Sunday is the **fuel** for Monday.

## The seven lenses, gathered up
- **Theology:** Communion is real union with Christ, the medicine of immortality (St Ignatius of Antioch).
- **The why:** we prepare not to "earn" it but because love comes *ready* and *awake* to the Beloved.
- **Connections:** the Mystical Supper (Holy Thursday) → every Liturgy; the chalice → the Cross and the
  empty tomb we receive the fruit of; "the least of these" → the Last Judgment (Matthew 25).
- **Beauty / lived Tradition:** crossed hands, the priest's blessing, the name spoken, the antidoron
  (blessed bread) shared at the end, even those not receiving share in the blessing and the fellowship.
- **Formation (the lead):** the Liturgy is meant to **make you** into someone, sent, peaceful, merciful,   not just inform you. *Worship → become → go and love.*

## How to live it this week
- **Make peace before the chalice.** Pick one person you're holding something against and **let it go**
  before next Sunday (this ties to your dad + Cleo). Reconciliation is the doorway to Communion.
- **Ask Fr Bogdan one practical question:** how he'd like you to prepare for Communion, fasting, the
  pre-Communion prayers, and Confession. Let *him* set the pace (it's pastoral).
- **Try "the liturgy after the Liturgy" once.** After church (or after prayer), do **one** deliberate act
  of mercy that day, carry the peace out the door.

## Closing prayer
> *Lord Jesus Christ, You give me Your very self in Your holy Mysteries. Make me worthy to receive You with
> the fear of God, with faith and love; cleanse my heart, make peace where I have broken it, and send me out
> to carry Your peace and mercy into all I meet. For You are the One we receive, and the One we go to serve.
> Amen.*
