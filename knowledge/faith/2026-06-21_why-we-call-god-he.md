# Why We Call God "He" (and what it does NOT mean)
**Date:** 2026-06-21  ·  **Track:** B (Theology). King's question.
*(Re-leveled 2026-06-27 to the new bar: toddler-simple, warm and human, with the truth to carry on your
heart. Quotes confirmed NKJV / OSB Septuagint.)*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
Here is a question lots of people wonder about. We always call God "He." Does that mean God is a man, a boy,
a male? No. Not at all. Let's clear it up simply.

Being a "he" or a "she," being male or female, is something that belongs to *created* things, like people and
animals and birds. But God is not a created thing. God *made* all of that. He is bigger than all of it. So God
is not male and not female. He is beyond the whole idea of boy-or-girl. He is not "a big man in the sky."

So why do we say "He" then? Two simple reasons.

The first reason: that is how God showed Himself to us. Jesus taught us to pray *"Our Father."* And God's own
Son came to earth as a real man, Jesus. So we use the names God actually *gave* us. We do not get to make up
our own names for God. We use His.

The second reason: saying "He" keeps God as a real *Someone* you can know and love, not a cold "it" or a
"force" like electricity. You cannot pray to an "it." You cannot love a force. But you can love a Father.
(In English we do not have a warm, personal word that is neither he nor she, so "He" is the closest we have
to keep God personal.)

And here is something tender: the Bible also shows God's love in a *motherly* way. Jesus said He longed to
gather His people *"as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings"* (Matthew 23:37). And God says, *"As one
whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you"* (Isaiah 66:13). So God's love has a father's strength *and*
a mother's tenderness, both. We still keep the names He gave, Father and Son, but His heart holds all of that
love.

This matters for you, King, because it means you can run to God as a real, loving Father, while never making
the mistake of thinking He is just a bigger, stronger version of a human man. He is far greater, and far more
loving, than that.

## In one line
God is not male. He is beyond boy-or-girl completely. We say "He" because that is how God showed Himself (as
Father, and the Son who became Jesus), and because "He" keeps God a personal Someone, not a cold "it."

## The lesson (slowly, step by step)
**1. God is not male.** Male and female belong to the things God *made*. God is the Maker, so He is beyond
all of that. Calling Him "He" does not mean He is masculine like a man.

**2. So why "He"?** Two reasons. First, *God revealed Himself that way*: Jesus taught us to pray *"Our Father
in heaven, hallowed be Your name"* (Matthew 6:9), and the Son became the man Jesus. We use the names God gave,
not ones we invent. Second, *it keeps God personal*: "He" guards that God is a *Who*, a Someone you can talk
to and love, not an "it."

**3. The wise teachers already knew the word doesn't trap God.** St Gregory the Theologian (in his Oration
31) gently mocked anyone who thought the word "Father" makes God male. He pointed out that in the Greek
language the word for "Deity" is actually a *feminine* word, and the word for "Spirit" is *neither* male nor
female. The gender of a *word* never decides what God actually *is.* The point of "Father" is love and source,
not maleness.

**4. God's love is fatherly and motherly both.** Scripture is not shy about this. The hen gathering her
chicks (Matthew 23:37). The mother comforting her child (Isaiah 66:13). God holds a father's strength and a
mother's tenderness together. The Church still keeps the revealed names, Father and Son, but knows His love
is fuller than any one human picture.

## Live it this week
The next time you pray, start the way Jesus taught: slowly say *"Our Father."* Let those two words land. You
are not talking to a force or a faraway king. You are talking to a Father who loves you with a strength and a
tenderness no human parent could ever match.
