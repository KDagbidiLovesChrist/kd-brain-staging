# How Do We Know God Is Real?
**Date:** 2026-06-21  ·  **Track:** E/B (Apologetics + Theology). King's own question.
*(Re-leveled 2026-06-27 to the new bar: toddler-simple, warm and human, with the truth to carry on your
heart. Quotes confirmed NKJV / OSB Septuagint.)*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
Here is a question almost everyone asks at some point: how do I even know God is real? Let's take it slow and
honest.

First, a true thing that might surprise you. There is no single magic proof that forces every person to
believe, the way 1 plus 1 forces you to say 2. And that is not God failing. As you will see at the end, there
is a *reason* for it, and it is a loving reason.

So how *do* we know? Think about how you know a *person* is real and good. Not by a maths test. You know them
by getting close: by what they have done, by spending time with them, by what it feels like to be near them.
That is how we come to know God too.

There are four little **signposts** that point toward God. A signpost does not drag you down the road. It
just points. Here they are, in plain words:
1. **Something exists instead of nothing.** There is a whole universe. It did not have to be here. Something
   must have started it all.
2. **The universe is set up just right for life.** Tiny settings (like how strong gravity is) are balanced
   so perfectly that if they were a hair off, nothing could live. That looks set up, like a thermostat tuned
   on purpose.
3. **We all feel a right and a wrong.** Deep down you know cruelty is *wrong*, not just "something you don't
   like." A real rule over everybody points to Somebody behind the rule.
4. **We ache for something this world never fills.** You can get everything you want and still feel "there
   must be more." That ache is like hunger. Hunger is a sign food exists. This hunger is a sign God does.

Those four point. The *strongest* thing for us Christians is bigger than a signpost. It is that God actually
**stepped into history as Jesus, and rose from the dead.** That really happened, and it can be looked into.

But the deepest answer of all, the Orthodox way (your Church's way), is this: you know God by **meeting Him.**
The Bible says, *"Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good"* (Psalm 34:8). You do not argue your way to
certain. You *taste*. You pray, you go to the Liturgy, you love people, and slowly you just *know* He is
there, the way you know your own dad is in the next room.

And why does God stay a little bit hidden, instead of writing His name across the sky? Because **love can
never be forced.** If God proved Himself so loudly that you *had* to believe, you could never freely choose
to love Him, and forced love is not love at all. So He stays close enough to be found, and quiet enough to
keep you free. This matters for you, King: you will not argue your way to peace about God. You will *know*
Him by living close to Him, exactly like Fr Bogdan is teaching you.

## In one line
We do not mostly *prove* God like a sum. We come to *know* Him like a Person: by signposts that point to
Him, by what He did in Jesus, and most of all by meeting Him ("taste and see").

## The lesson (slowly, step by step)
**1. An honest start.** No single proof forces everyone, and pretending otherwise is not faith, it is just
pressure. What we have is *lots* of evidence pointing the same way, *plus* a different kind of knowing:
meeting God for yourself. So the real question is not "can I prove God in one sentence?" It is "where does
all of it point, once I actually start walking toward Him?"

**2. The four signposts (they point, they do not shove).** Existence, the fine-tuning, the moral law, and
the deep ache (above). Smart people push back on each one (maybe the universe just *is*, maybe there are
billions of other universes, maybe right-and-wrong is just instinct), and that is fair. None of these *force*
belief. But put together, God is the simplest answer that ties all four loose threads into one knot. (Honest
limit: these get you to "a powerful, good Mind behind it all," not yet to Jesus by name. For *that*, we need
history.)

**3. The big one: Jesus really rose.** Christianity does not just say "a god exists." It says *this* God came
as Jesus and proved it by rising from the dead, in real history you can check. Even non-Christian scholars
agree on the bedrock facts: Jesus truly lived and was crucified. His friends were *sure* they saw Him alive
again, and they died rather than take it back (you do not die for something you know you made up). The Church
exploded in the very city He was killed in. And enemies became believers, like Paul, who had been hunting
Christians. The earliest summary of all this was written down within just a few years of it happening, far
too soon to be a slow-grown legend. Paul bet everything on it: *"if Christ is not risen, your faith is
futile; you are still in your sins!"* (1 Corinthians 15:17). (Honest limit: history can show the facts. It
cannot force you to say "so, a miracle." That last step is a choice. A skeptic can weigh it differently, and
that is allowed.)

**4. The deepest way, the Orthodox heart: you KNOW God by meeting Him.** *"Oh, taste and see that the Lord is
good"* (Psalm 34:8). This is an invitation, not a sum. You do not just read God's file, you meet Him: in
prayer, in Communion, in the lives of the saints (people so full of God it shines out of them). One holy man,
St Seraphim of Sarov, taught that the whole point of the Christian life is "the acquisition of the Holy
Spirit." The proof shows up *in changed people.* (How can a tiny human meet the infinite God? St Gregory
Palamas explained it with the sun: you can never grab the sun's burning core, but its light and warmth really
do reach you and touch your skin, and that light truly *is* the sun. God's deepest self stays beyond us, but
His grace, His warmth, His life, really reaches us.)

## The honest, hard part (faced, not dodged)
A real answer does not run from the hard questions:
- **"Why doesn't God just appear?"** Because love cannot be forced (see above). *"Behold, I stand at the door
  and knock"* (Revelation 3:20). He knocks. He does not kick the door in.
- **Faith is not a blind jump.** It is *trust*, the kind you give a person you have come to know. *"Now faith
  is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen"* (Hebrews 11:1).
- **Doubt can live inside faith.** The most honest prayer in the whole Gospel is *"Lord, I believe; help my
  unbelief!"* (Mark 9:24), and Jesus helped the man anyway. So your doubts do not push you out.
- **Suffering is not explained away.** The faith does not hand you a neat reason that makes a cancer ward
  "fine." It gives you a God who *climbs in* and suffers with us. On the Cross, Jesus Himself cried, *"My God,
  My God, why have You forsaken Me?"* God knows it from the inside.
- **The doubter is treated with respect.** "A God who wanted to be known could just show up" is a serious,
  honest objection, not stupidity. Some people sincerely look and feel only silence. We hold that gently. And
  notice: God *did* meet some people head-on (Paul, and doubting Thomas), so "He never forces" is His usual
  way, not an iron rule.
- **In the end it is an invitation, not an argument.** When the doubter Nathanael scoffed, *"Can anything
  good come out of Nazareth?"*, the answer was not a debate. It was two words: *"Come and see"* (John 1:46).
  He only believed after he met Jesus.
