# Icons and Veneration · how and why we honour the saints and the Theotokos · ② The Tradition

**Date:** 2026-06-28  ·  **Track:** D Praxis (the Orthodox way, how the faith is lived)

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
An **icon** is a holy picture of Christ, His mother, or a saint. First thing to settle: we do **not** pray to
the wood and paint, and we do **not** think the picture is God. An icon is like a **window**, or like a **photo
of someone you love**. When you kiss a photo of your mum, you are not in love with paper. The love travels
through the photo to your mum. That is exactly what we do with an icon. We **honour** the person shown (the
Church calls this **veneration**), and the honour passes through the picture to them. **Worship** (giving your
whole self the way you only give it to God) belongs to **God alone**. And we are allowed to make these holy
pictures because of the biggest event in history: in Jesus, **God became a man you could see and touch**.
Before that, no one could picture God. After that, God has a face. This matters to King because every morning
and evening he now **bows before the icons of Christ and His mother**, and this lesson says exactly what he is
doing and why it is good and right.

## In one line
We do not worship icons; we honour the holy ones they show, and the honour passes through to them, because in
Christ the invisible God became visible.

## ✨ Carry this on your heart
**"I do not worship wood and paint. I kiss the window, and the love passes to the One it shows."**
*Little prayer: "Lord, You became visible for me. Let every icon turn my heart to You."*

## The lesson

**First, the honest worry, said fairly.** Some Christians, especially many Protestants, see a person bow and
kiss a picture and think, *"That is idol worship; that breaks the commandment."* This is a **sincere,
good-hearted** worry, because God truly hates idolatry. So we answer it honestly, not dodge it.

**Two different words English mashes into one.** The Church keeps two things apart. **Worship** (Greek
*latreia*) = the total adoration that belongs to **God alone**. **Veneration** (Greek *proskynesis* = honour,
a bow of respect) = the respect we show to **holy people and things**. English crushes both into "worship,"
and that is where confusion is born. We give *latreia* to **God only**; we give **honour** to the saints and
icons. Completely different acts of the heart.

**What the commandment actually forbids.** The Second Commandment forbids making a carved image and bowing to
it **as a god** (Ex 20:4-5). It forbids **false gods**, not all images. Proof: a few chapters later the **same
God** tells Moses to **make images** — two **golden cherubim** on the Ark, in the holiest place (Ex 25:18-22),
angels woven in the temple veil, and later Solomon's temple **filled** with carved angels, palms and flowers
(1 Kings 6). God even had Moses make a **bronze serpent** people looked to and were healed (Num 21). So God
forbids **idols**, not **sacred images**. The line is in the heart: treating it as a god, or honouring the true
God through it.

**The key that unlocks everything: the Incarnation.** (*Incarnation* = God the Son becoming a real human in
Jesus.) In the Old Testament you could not picture God; no one had seen Him and He had no body. Then **"the
Word became flesh and dwelt among us"** (John 1:14). Now God has a human face. **St John of Damascus** (who
defended icons): *"I do not worship matter; I worship the God of matter, who became matter for my sake."*
Because God Himself took a body, we can show that body. Refusing to paint Christ almost denies He **really**
became man. So icons quietly **guard** the truth of the Incarnation.

**"The honour passes to the prototype."** (*Prototype* = the real person the picture shows.) **St Basil the
Great:** *"the honour given to the image passes to the prototype."* A soldier salutes the flag and the respect
goes to the **country**, not the cloth. Kiss the icon of Christ and the kiss goes to **Christ**.

**The Church settled it forever.** (*Ecumenical Council* = a gathering of the whole Church's bishops to settle a
question.) Some had smashed icons, this was **iconoclasm** ("icon-smashing"). At the **7th Ecumenical Council,
Nicaea, 787**, the Church restored icons and made the rule plain: icons receive **honour / veneration**, but
**not** *latreia* (worship), which is God's alone. Still celebrated yearly on the **Sunday of Orthodoxy**.

**Why we honour the saints and ask their prayers.** (*Saint* = a holy person who lived in Christ and is now
with Him. *Communion of saints* = the one family of God, living and departed, joined in Christ.) The saints are
**not dead and gone**; Jesus said God *"is not the God of the dead, but of the living"* (Matt 22:32). They are
**more alive** than us. We honour them as **older brothers and sisters who finished the race** (Heb 12:1, *"so
great a cloud of witnesses"*) and **ask their prayers**, just as you'd ask a friend or Fr Bogdan to pray. If
asking a living friend to pray is good (James 5:16), asking one now **face to face with God** is better. Heaven
shows them holding **golden bowls of incense, "which are the prayers of the saints"** (Rev 5:8). We do not pray
to them as gods; we ask their prayers, like nudging a big brother to put in a word with our Father.

**The Theotokos.** (*Theotokos* = "God-bearer," Mary, the mother of God; *theo* = God, *tokos* = bearer.) We
honour her **above all** saints, because she **carried God Himself** in her body. She said it: *"all generations
will call me blessed"* (Luke 1:48). We do **not** worship her — she is a creature, not God — but we love and
honour her as the highest saint and ask her prayers, because no one is closer to Jesus than His own mother (at
Cana, *"Do whatever He tells you,"* John 2, and He did).

## The seven lenses (God the Author at work)
- **Typology, the Ark of the Covenant → the Theotokos.** The Ark (a gold box) held the **tablets of the
  Word**, the **manna** (bread from heaven), and **Aaron's rod** (the priest); God's presence rested over it.
  Mary held the real **Word** (Jesus), the true **Bread of Heaven**, the true **High Priest**, the **living
  Ark**. The craft across 1,000 years: David — *"How can the Ark of the Lord come to me?"* (2 Sam 6:9);
  Elizabeth — *"Why is this granted to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?"* (Luke 1:43). Luke
  wrote it so we would see it.
- **Wordplay in the word "icon."** Christ is *"the image of the invisible God"* (Col 1:15); the Greek for
  "image" is **eikōn** = icon. We make icons only because **God first made Himself an icon (visible) for us.**
- **The two held together.** The God who said *"no idols"* (Ex 20) **commanded** golden angels in His holiest
  place (Ex 25). Reading Scripture **whole**, not one verse torn out, is discernment.

  (carved cherubim in the temple); Num 21:8-9 (the bronze serpent); **John 1:14** (the Word made flesh);

- Lived Tradition: the Sunday of Orthodoxy (the restoration of icons); home icons / prayer corner; bowing,
  the sign of the Cross, kissing the icon; asking the prayers of the saints and the Theotokos.

## Live it this week
Tomorrow morning, before the prostrations, **pause one second** at the icon of Christ and say in your heart:
*"This is the face of my God, who became visible for me."* Let the icon stop being a picture on the wall and
become a **window** you look through to Him. One slow, aware breath turns the rule from routine into a meeting.

## Prayer (the Troparion of the Sunday of Orthodoxy)
*We venerate Your most pure image, O Good One, and ask forgiveness of our transgressions, O Christ our God; for
by Your own will You were pleased to ascend the Cross in the flesh, to deliver from slavery to the enemy those
whom You had fashioned. Therefore we cry to You with thanksgiving: You have filled all things with joy, O our
Saviour, for You came to save the world. Through the prayers of the Theotokos and all the saints. Amen.*
