# Matthew 3 · John the Baptist and the Baptism of Christ (Theophany)

*The Truth project · ① The Word · Gospel of Matthew, chapter 3 · Lesson #26-2026-06-26*
*Taught OSB-style (study notes for the whole chapter), with the seven lenses, baby-simple + story-flow.*
*Scripture quoted **NKJV** (King's Orthodox Study Bible NT). Every quote verified.*

> *"This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." — Matthew 3:17 (NKJV)*

---

## Where we are
Matthew 1 = Jesus' family tree + His birth ("Jesus = God saves," Emmanuel). Matthew 2 = the Magi, wicked
Herod, the flight to Egypt, growing up in Nazareth (Jesus the new Moses). Now the curtain jumps ahead
**about thirty years**, Jesus is a grown man, and the story of His public work begins. And it begins not
with Jesus, but with a wild man in the desert shouting one word: **Repent.**

## The chapter as a story

### 1) The voice in the wilderness (3:1-6)
A man named **John the Baptist** appears, preaching in the **wilderness of Judea**, the dry, empty
desert country. ("**The Baptist**" = "the baptiser," from his work of baptising people in the river.) His
message is short and burning: *"Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand!"* (3:2).
- **Repent** (Greek *metanoia*) doesn't mean just "feel sorry." It means **turn your whole self around**,   change your mind, change your direction, turn back to God. Picture walking the wrong way down a road and
  doing a full U-turn.
- **"The kingdom of heaven is at hand"** = God's reign, God's King, is *right here, about to arrive*. Get
  ready.

Matthew tells us John is the one the prophet **Isaiah** spoke of 700 years earlier: *"The voice of one
crying in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the LORD; make His paths straight'"* (3:3, quoting Isaiah
40:3). In the old days, before a king travelled, workers went ahead to clear and smooth the road. **John
is the road-crew for the King.** That's his title: the **Forerunner**, the one who runs ahead.

John even *looks* the part: **camel's hair clothing**, a leather belt, eating **locusts and wild honey**
(3:4). To a Jewish reader this is a flashing sign: that's exactly how the great prophet **Elijah** dressed
(2 Kings 1:8). The prophets had said Elijah would come back before the Messiah — so Matthew is saying
**John comes "in the spirit of Elijah,"** the last of the old prophets, pointing to the new King. Crowds
stream out from Jerusalem and all around the Jordan, confessing their sins and being **baptised** by him
in the river (3:5, 6), a washing in water that stands for a washed-out, turned-around life.

### 2) "Brood of vipers" · real change, not a show (3:7-10)
Two groups come out to watch: the **Pharisees** and the **Sadducees**.
- **Pharisees** = the very religious, rule-keeping group, strict about the Law and proud of it.
- **Sadducees** = the priestly, upper-class group tied to the Temple, who didn't even believe in the
  resurrection.
They're rivals who agree on almost nothing, but John treats both the same. He calls them a **"brood of
vipers"** (a nest of baby snakes!) and warns them: don't think being *born* the right race ("we have
Abraham as our father") saves you (3:7, 9). *"Bear fruits worthy of repentance"* (3:8), **prove** the
turn-around by how you actually live. And the warning has teeth: *"the axe is laid to the root of the
trees… every tree which does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire"* (3:10). God wants
**fruit**, not labels.

### 3) "One mightier than I is coming" (3:11-12)
John is careful to make himself small: *"I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is
coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the
**Holy Spirit and fire**"* (3:11). Carrying a master's sandals was a slave's lowest job, John says he's
not even fit for *that*.
- **John's baptism** = water, a sign of repentance, getting people ready.
- **Christ's baptism** = the real thing: the **Holy Spirit** poured into you, and **fire** that burns away
  what's dead and warms what's alive. (This is fulfilled at Pentecost and given to us in Holy Baptism +
  Chrismation in the Church.)

### 4) The Baptism of Christ · the Jordan (3:13-17)
Then **Jesus Himself** walks down from Galilee to the Jordan to be baptised by John. John is stunned and
tries to stop Him: *"I need to be baptized by You, and are You coming to me?"* (3:14). **Of course**, John
baptises *sinners* turning back to God, and **Jesus has no sin.** So why does the sinless One get in the
water?

Jesus answers: *"Permit it to be so now, for thus it is fitting for us to **fulfill all righteousness**"*
(3:15). He isn't being washed *from* sin, **He has none.** He steps into our place, into our dirty river,
to **stand with us** and to **begin His rescue from the inside**. The Fathers say it beautifully: Jesus
doesn't get clean *in* the water, **He makes the water clean.** He sanctifies the waters, and through them
all the waters of our own Baptism. He goes down into the Jordan the way He will later go down into death, to bless it from within and lead us up.

And then **heaven opens** (3:16, 17). Three things happen at once:
1. **The Son** stands in the water, God the Son, in our flesh.
2. **The Holy Spirit** descends "like a dove" and rests on Him.
3. **The Father's voice** comes from heaven: *"This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased"* (3:17).

This moment has a name: **Theophany** (Greek *theos* = God, *phaino* = to appear), **"the appearing of
God."** Also called **Epiphany** ("the showing forth"). For the **first time in the whole Bible, the Holy
Trinity is revealed all at once, openly**, Father (the voice), Son (in the Jordan), Holy Spirit (the
dove). Everything you learned about God being *one God in three Persons*, here it is, shown at the river.

## The seven lenses, gathered up
- **Theology:** the Trinity made manifest; Christ identifying with sinners He came to save; the start of
  His public ministry, "anointed" by the Spirit (this is what "**Christ / Messiah** = the Anointed One"
  means, anointed here not with oil but with the Spirit Himself).
- **The why:** the sinless One is baptised not to be cleansed but to **cleanse the waters** and to take His
  place beside us, humility from the very first step.
- **Foreshadowing / typology:** going *down* into the Jordan → going *down* into death (the Cross and the
  Harrowing of Hades) and rising. The Jordan also recalls Israel crossing the Jordan into the Promised
  Land, Christ opens the way into the true Kingdom. Our own **Baptism** is us joined to *this* moment.
- **Connections:** the **dove** echoes Noah's dove after the flood (a new-creation sign, Genesis 8) and the
  Spirit "hovering over the waters" at creation (Genesis 1:2) — Baptism is a **new creation**. John as
  **Elijah** ties the last prophet to the first day of the Gospel.
- **Literary craft:** Matthew builds it, desert, voice, crowds, warning, then the King arrives and heaven
  itself speaks. The Father's words echo Psalm 2:7 ("You are My Son") and Isaiah 42:1 (the beloved
  Servant), King *and* suffering Servant in one breath.
- **Beauty / lived Tradition:** the whole Church sings this every year at **Theophany (January 6)** with
  the **Great Blessing of the Waters**, the priest blesses water (often a river, lake or the sea), and
  the faithful take holy water home. The feast's **troparion** (its main hymn) is the lesson in song:
  > *"When You, O Lord, were baptized in the Jordan, the worship of the Trinity was made manifest; for the
  > voice of the Father bore witness to You, calling You His beloved Son. And the Spirit, in the form of a
  > dove, confirmed the truthfulness of His word. O Christ our God, who appeared and enlightened the world,
  > glory to You."* (Apolytikion of Theophany, Tone 1, OCA)

## How to live it this week
- **Pick one "U-turn."** Repentance isn't a mood, it's a turn. Choose **one** concrete thing to turn from
  this week and *do* the turn, "fruit worthy of repentance," not just a feeling. (The struggle you've
  named before, bring it to **Fr Bogdan in Confession**; that's the Church's medicine, not mine to give.)
- **Remember your own Baptism.** You were joined to Christ in the water. When the accuser says "you don't
  belong," answer: *"I was baptised into Christ, I belong to Him."*
- **Make yourself small like John.** One small act of humility, let someone else be right, take the lower
  place once.

## Closing prayer
> *Lord Jesus Christ, who for our sake went down into the Jordan and sanctified the waters, and over whom
> the Father's voice was heard and the Spirit rested as a dove: cleanse me as You cleansed the waters, turn
> my heart back to You, and let me bear good fruit. Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy
> Spirit. Amen.*
