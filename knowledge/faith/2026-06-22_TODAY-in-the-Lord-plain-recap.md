## PART 2 · The big truths we learned (explained plainly, with Bible + people + history)

### Truth 1 · The opening of Matthew teaches that God keeps His promises
The New Testament begins with a long family tree ("so-and-so was the father of so-and-so"). It looks
boring, but it's a **receipt**. For 2,000 years God had promised two men that the Saviour would come
through their family:
- **Abraham** (about 2000 BC), God promised *"in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed"*
  (Genesis 12:3). Abraham is the father of the Jewish people and "the father of all who believe."
- **King David** (about 1000 BC), God promised one of his descendants a throne *forever*
  (2 Samuel 7:12–13).

The family tree proves Jesus is the real heir of both. **Why it matters:** Jesus is not a made-up legend.
He was born into real history, at the exact point all the old promises were aiming. God said it, and
2,000 years later He did it.

### Truth 2 · The name "Jesus" tells you His whole mission
An angel told Joseph: *"thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins"*
(Matthew 1:21). The name **Jesus** (in Hebrew, *Yeshua*) literally means **"the LORD saves."** And the
angel says what He saves us *from*, **our sins.**
- This is why the **Jesus Prayer** ("Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner") is so
  powerful, the very name you say *is* God's salvation. The night you fought temptation, you were holding
  onto the Name that saves people from exactly that.
- The chapter also calls Him **"Emmanuel," which means "God with us"** (Matthew 1:23) — a prophecy spoken
  700 years earlier by the prophet **Isaiah** (Isaiah 7:14). So Jesus is both fully a real human being (a
  real family, a real mother) and fully God. The Church calls this the **Incarnation** (see glossary).

### Truth 3 · A real teacher of the Church showed us God's mercy in that family tree
The family tree quietly lists **four women** who were sinners or foreigners (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and
Bathsheba). **St John Chrysostom**, a bishop who lived around AD 349, 407, nicknamed *"the golden-mouthed"*
because he preached so beautifully, wrote a whole set of sermons on Matthew. He marvels that Jesus was
**not ashamed** to have sinners and outsiders in His family, *because He came to save sinners and
outsiders.* That's the Gospel hidden in a list of names: **God's mercy reaches the broken and the
foreigner.** (This is also why we don't read the Bible alone, a wise teacher of the Church opened up a
meaning we'd have walked right past. More on that in Part 4.)

### Truth 4 · The Divine Liturgy is where heaven touches earth
The **Divine Liturgy** is the main Sunday worship service of the Orthodox Church, the service where we
receive **Holy Communion** (the Body and Blood of Christ). We learned three reasons the Church has it:
- **Jesus commanded it.** At His last supper He said *"this do in remembrance of me"* (Luke 22:19).
- **He said we truly need it.** *"Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, ye have
  no life in you"* (John 6:53). Orthodoxy believes Him literally — it really is His Body and Blood.
- **It's how we're united to God**, He doesn't just give us teachings, He gives us *Himself*.

**The history:** this isn't new. **St Ignatius of Antioch**, a bishop who was taught by the Apostle John
himself, and who was killed for Christ around AD 107, already called Communion *"the medicine of
immortality."* That's within a lifetime of Jesus. And around AD 988, when **Prince Vladimir of Kiev** sent
men to find the true faith, they attended the Liturgy and said: *"We knew not whether we were in heaven or
on earth."* That's how the Liturgy is meant to feel, **heaven on earth.**

## PART 3 · Plain-English glossary (the "advanced" words we used)
- **Liturgy**, from Greek *leitourgia*, "the work of the people." The Church's main worship service that
  everyone *does together* (not a show you watch).
- **Divine Liturgy**, the specific service where we receive Holy Communion. The most common one is the
  **Liturgy of St John Chrysostom** (used most Sundays for ~1,600 years).
- **Eucharist**, from Greek for "thanksgiving." Another name for Holy Communion.
- **Holy Communion / Body and Blood**, the bread and wine that, in the Liturgy, become the real Body and
  Blood of Christ, which we receive.
- **Incarnation** — God the Son becoming a real human being in Jesus ("the Word was made flesh," John 1:14).
- **Theosis**, "becoming like God / being united to God." The Orthodox word for the goal of salvation:
  not just being forgiven, but being filled with God's life (2 Peter 1:4, "partakers of the divine nature").
- **Theotokos**, "God-bearer," the title for Mary, because she carried God-in-the-flesh. (We meet her as
  the fifth woman in Matthew 1.)
- **Typology / foreshadowing**, when something in the Old Testament is a "preview" of Christ. Example: the
  Passover lamb whose blood saved Israel (Exodus 12) was a preview of Jesus, "the Lamb of God."
- **Trisagion**, "Thrice-Holy." The prayer *"Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us,"*
  said three times. You prayed it this morning.
- **Anaphora**, the great offering prayer in the Liturgy ("Thine own of Thine own we offer unto Thee").
- **Epiclesis**, "calling down." The moment the priest asks the Holy Spirit to change the bread and wine
  into Christ's Body and Blood.
- **Nepsis / watchfulness**, guarding your thoughts; catching a temptation at the very first moment, before
  it grows. Exactly what you did the night before. **St John Climacus** (a monk, c.AD 579, 649) taught this
  in his book *The Ladder of Divine Ascent*.
- **Apologetics**, from Greek *apologia*, "a defense." Explaining and defending the faith, gently, when
  someone asks or challenges it (1 Peter 3:15).
- **The Fathers (Church Fathers)**, the holy, trusted teachers and bishops of the early Church (like
  Ignatius, Athanasius, Chrysostom) whose teaching the whole Church recognises as faithful.
- **Ecumenical Councils**, seven great worldwide meetings of the bishops (AD 325, 787) that settled the
  faith and answered false teachings. (We'll study these later; this is where we'll "meet" the heresies.)

## PART 4 · "The Bible is not the only way we learn God" (Holy Tradition)
You said this, and it is deeply Orthodox and true. Here's why, plainly:
- **The Church came BEFORE the New Testament.** Jesus never handed out a book; He started a Church
  (Matthew 16:18). The first Christians worshipped, took Communion, and lived the faith for ~20 years
  before the first Gospel was even written, and ~300 years before the Bible was gathered into one book.
- **The Bible itself says it's not the only source.** St Paul wrote: *"hold the traditions which ye have
  been taught, whether by word, or our epistle"* (2 Thessalonians 2:15) — **by word OR by letter.** And the
  Apostle John said not everything Jesus did was written down (John 21:25).
- **The Bible says the CHURCH is what guards the truth:** *"the church of the living God, the pillar and
  ground of the truth"* (1 Timothy 3:15). The Bible is the heart of the Church's Tradition, read *inside*
  the Church, not alone.

**So how DO we come to know God?** Through all of these together (this is "Holy Tradition"):
1. **Holy Scripture**, the Bible (the written heart of it).
2. **The Liturgy and the sacraments**, worship, and especially Holy Communion, where we *meet* Him.
3. **The Church Fathers**, the trusted teachers who explain Scripture rightly (like Chrysostom today).
4. **The Ecumenical Councils**, the Church together guarding the truth.
5. **The icons and the saints' lives**, God shown and lived out in real people.
6. **Prayer and encounter**, actually meeting God in the heart (the Jesus Prayer, stillness).
7. **Creation itself** — *"the heavens declare the glory of God"* (Psalm 19:1).

The "test" for all of it: it must agree with the **consensus of the Fathers** and the Councils, the one
voice of the Church across 2,000 years. Bring the big questions to **Fr Bogdan.**
