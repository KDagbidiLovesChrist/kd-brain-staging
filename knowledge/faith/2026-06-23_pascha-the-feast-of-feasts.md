# Pascha: The Feast of Feasts, What It Means
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** ⛪ Lived Tradition (the Church year) · *born from King's question*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
King asked what Pascha means. Pascha is the Orthodox word for Easter, and it is the very same word as "Passover." It is the greatest day of the whole Christian year, so important the Church calls it "the Feast of Feasts." It celebrates the Resurrection, Jesus rising from the dead and trampling death by going through it. The name "Passover" connects it to the old story: long ago in Egypt, God's people were slaves, and on the night of the Passover a lamb was killed and its blood put on the doorposts, so that death "passed over" those homes and the people passed out of slavery into freedom. The Church says Jesus is the true Passover Lamb: by His blood, death passes over us, and He brings us out of the slavery of sin and death into the freedom of life with God. So Pascha is not just remembering a happy ending; it is OUR passage from death to life. The whole faith stands on it: Saint Paul said if Christ is not risen, our faith is useless, but He IS risen. That is why at the midnight service Orthodox Christians light candles in the dark and shout "Christ is risen!" and answer "Indeed He is risen!" This matters for King because everything he is learning flows out of this one victory, and his own death has already been turned into a doorway by it.

## In one line
Pascha (Passover / Easter) is the Feast of Feasts, the celebration of Christ's Resurrection, the true Passover Lamb whose death and rising carry us from the slavery of sin and death into the freedom of life with God; the whole faith stands or falls on it.

## The teaching

**1. The word: Pascha = Passover.** "Pascha" comes straight from the Hebrew *Pesach*, Passover. So Easter and Passover are deliberately the same word, because the new fulfills the old. *"Christ, our Passover (Pascha), was sacrificed for us"* (1 Corinthians 5:7, NKJV).

**2. The old Passover (the picture God set up in advance).** In Egypt God's people were slaves. On the night of deliverance (Exodus 12), each family killed a lamb without blemish and put its blood on the doorposts; the angel of death "passed over" the blood-marked homes, and that night the people passed out of slavery, through the Red Sea, into freedom. It was a rescue *through* blood and *through* water, from death and slavery into life and freedom.

**3. The new and true Passover (Christ).** Jesus is the true, spotless **Lamb of God** (John 1:29). His blood is the real blood on the doorposts of the world; by it, death passes over us. He leads us out of the slavery of **sin and death** into the freedom of life with God, and through the water of **baptism** (the new Red Sea) we pass from death to life. Pascha is the great Exodus brought to its fulfillment.

**4. What we actually celebrate: the Resurrection and the trampling of death.** Pascha is the day Christ rose, having gone down into death and shattered it from within (see the Hades lesson). The Paschal hymn says it all: *"Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life."*

**5. Why it is the Feast of Feasts.** It is the center of the entire Christian year and of the faith itself. Everything else, every other feast, the whole Gospel, leans on this one fact. *"If Christ is not risen, your faith is futile"* (1 Corinthians 15:17), *"but now Christ is risen from the dead"* (15:20). The fasting of Great Lent leads up to it; the joy of Pascha overflows for weeks after.

**6. How it is lived (beauty and formation).** At the midnight service the church is dark, then the light of the risen Christ is shared candle to candle until the whole church glows; the people proclaim *"Christ is risen!"* and answer *"Indeed He is risen!"* (in Greek, *Christos anesti! Alithos anesti!*). It is pure joy, heaven breaking into earth. This is the Pascha you will one day stand in at your own parish with Fr Bogdan.

## The backing (verified)
- 1 Corinthians 5:7 (Christ our Passover); John 1:29 (the Lamb of God); Exodus 12 (the first Passover); 1 Corinthians 15:17, 15:20 (the faith stands on the Resurrection) (NKJV; Exodus per OSB).
- The Paschal troparion: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life." (standard liturgical text)
- The Paschal greeting "Christ is risen! / Indeed He is risen!" (universal Orthodox practice).

## Live it this week
You do not have to wait for Easter to live in Pascha. When fear or sin or the thought of death weighs on you, say the Paschal words: *"Christ is risen, trampling down death by death."* The Resurrection is not only a day on the calendar; it is the air a Christian breathes.

## Prayer
*O Christ our Pascha, our true Passover Lamb, by Your death You trampled death and by Your rising You opened life to me. Lead me out of the slavery of my sins into Your freedom, and let me live as one who knows that You are risen. Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner. Amen.*
