# Beginning the Day with God · should faith be the first thing every morning?
**Date:** 2026-06-23  ·  **Track:** Lived Tradition / Formation (the Orthodox Way) · *born from King's own question*

## 📖 In plain words (the easy version)
Should God be the first thing you do each morning? The Church's answer is yes, give God the **first-fruits** of your day, meaning the *first* and best of your attention, not the leftovers after everything else has used you up. Jesus said to "seek first" God's Kingdom, and when you start with Him your whole day gets pointed at Him; start with the phone or the worry, and the day gets pointed at *that* instead. This isn't a box to tick out of fear, it's like reaching for someone you love before the busy day pulls you away. The honest caution is to keep your **rule** (your set little routine of prayer) small and doable: two faithful minutes every day beats twenty minutes you dread and quit. And if you miss a morning, that's not failure, you just gently begin again, and you let **Fr Bogdan** (King's spiritual father) set the right pace. This matters for King because it turns each day into a relationship with God from the very first moment, kept gentle so it stays a love and not a performance.

## In one line
Yes, the Church's deep and consistent wisdom is to give God the **first-fruits** of your day; but the *shape* of your morning rule is set gently, kept small and faithful, and guided by Fr Bogdan, it's a relationship, not a performance.

## The lesson

**1. "Seek ye first."** Christ gave the ordering principle Himself: *"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you"* (Matthew 6:33). What you put *first* orders everything after it. Begin with God and the whole day is oriented around Him; begin with the phone, the news, the worry, and the day is oriented around *that*.

**2. First-fruits, not leftovers.** Scripture's pattern is to give God the *first* and best, not what's left over: *"Honour the Lord… with the firstfruits of all thine increase"* (Proverbs 3:9). Your first waking attention is a kind of first-fruit; giving it to God says, in actions, "You are first."

**3. Christ did it; the psalmist longed for it.** *"And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out… and there prayed"* (Mark 1:35). *"My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O Lord; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee"* (Psalm 5:3); *"O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee"* (Psalm 63:1). The morning turn to God runs right through the Tradition — it's why the Orthodox prayer book *has* a set of **Morning Prayers** as a normal part of the rule.

**4. It's relationship, not a box to tick.** This is the heart of it. You pray first not out of fear of punishment but because you turn to the One you love first, the way you'd reach for someone dear before the day pulls you away.

**5. The honest caution (this is Fr Bogdan's part).** The *principle* is yes; the *shape* of your rule, how long, which prayers, is what a spiritual father guides:
- **Start small and keepable.** The Orthodox way is gentleness: a short rule you keep every day beats a long one you abandon in a week. Better two faithful minutes than twenty you dread.
- **Avoid two traps:** *legalism* (guilt when you miss, that's the accuser; see the humility lesson) and *all-or-nothing* perfectionism. A missed morning is not failure; you simply begin again.
- **Bring your actual morning rule to Fr Bogdan**, let him set the pace, so it stays a love and not a performance.

## Live it this week
Before the phone, before anything, the **sign of the cross** and one short line as the very first act of the day: *"Glory to Thee, O God"* or the Jesus Prayer, *"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner."* Then your morning reading. Build from there, with Fr Bogdan. When you miss a morning, don't condemn yourself, just begin again.

## Prayer
*Lord, before this day takes me anywhere, let it take me to You first. Be my first thought and my last. Order my hours around You, and teach me to seek Your Kingdom first. Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner. Amen.*
