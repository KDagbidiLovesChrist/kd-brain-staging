# Christian Astrology · What Is It, and What Does the Church Teach?

## What Is "Christian Astrology"?

The term appears in two places:

**1. William Lilly's "Christian Astrology" (1647):** The most famous English-language astrology textbook. The "Christian" label was cultural, not theological, distinguishing it from "pagan" or "Arabic" astrology. A technical manual for horoscope-casting. No church endorsed it.

**2. Modern syncretic practice:** People combining Christian identity with astrological belief. Very common in culture; firmly contrary to Church teaching.

Neither represents the Church's position.

## What Scripture Says

**Isaiah 47:13-14 (NKJV):**
> "You are wearied in the multitude of your counsels; let now the astrologers, the stargazers, and the monthly prognosticators stand up and save you from what shall come upon you. Behold, they shall be as stubble, the fire shall burn them; they shall not deliver themselves from the power of the flame."

God mocking Babylon's astrologers. "Monthly prognosticators" = those who predicted the future from the stars each month. Verdict: powerless.

**Deuteronomy 18:10-12 (NKJV):**
> "There shall not be found among you anyone who makes his son or his daughter pass through the fire, or one who practices witchcraft, or a soothsayer, or one who interprets omens, or a sorcerer... For all who do these things are an abomination to the LORD."

"Soothsayer" and "one who interprets omens" covers astrological divination. "Abomination" is not mild.

**Daniel 2:** Nebuchadnezzar called all of Babylon's astrologers and wise men to tell him his dream and interpret it. They could not. Daniel could, because God revealed it. The contrast is the point: the entire apparatus of Babylonian astrology is powerless. God alone knows what is coming.

## Church Fathers and Councils

**St. Basil the Great (c. 329-379):** In his *Hexaemeron* (homilies on the six days of creation), Basil teaches that the stars are created things, God-made lights to mark times and seasons (Genesis 1:14-18), not governors of human fate. He refused astrological determinism because it destroys free will and personal responsibility before God.

**St. Augustine (354-430):** In his *Confessions*, he describes his own earlier attraction to astrology and his conversion away from it. In *The City of God* (Book V), he makes the twin argument: if the stars determine your destiny at birth, why do twins born minutes apart live completely different lives? His conclusion: God's providence and human free will explain human lives; the stars do not. (Augustine is a Western Father; Orthodoxy reads him with nuance, but his logic here is sound and widely cited.)

**The Council in Trullo / Quinisext Council (692 AD), Canon 61:** Authoritative in the Eastern Orthodox Church. Condemns those who consult diviners, soothsayers, and those who practice "the sorcery of divination from birth" (natal astrology). Penalty: six years of penance/censure.

**The Council of Laodicea (4th century), Canon 36:** Forbade clergy from practicing divination or magical arts, inseparable from astrology in the ancient world.

## The Theological WHY

**1. Free will.** Orthodox theology affirms genuine human freedom. You are not a machine running a programme written in the stars. Astrology says birth-position of planets tells you who you are and what will happen. These two claims cannot both be true.

**2. God's providence.** Matthew 10:29-30 (NKJV): *"Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered."* God governs personally, in love. Going to the stars for guidance bypasses the actual Person who holds your life.

**3. Creature over Creator.** Romans 1:25 (NKJV): *"who exchanged the truth of God for the lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator."* Seeking destiny in created stars is exactly this move. The stars are beautiful. They are not wisdom-givers.

**4. Divination is always forbidden.** Astrology is divination: accessing hidden knowledge of the future through means other than God. Forbidden consistently across both Testaments. Not harshness, but protection: it pulls people from relationship with God toward created things.

## The Magi Question (Matthew 2)

The most common objection: if the Magi followed a star, doesn't this endorse astrology?

No. Orthodox reading:

- The Magi were Gentile wise men from the East (likely Persia/Babylon). God met them where they were, using a sign in the sky they would notice.
- But the star told them very little: something significant happened, royally, toward Judea. They had to go to Jerusalem and ask: *"Where is He who has been born King of the Jews?"* (Matthew 2:2 NKJV).
- The chief priests and scribes gave them the specific location, from the prophet Micah (Micah 5:2). **Scripture completed what the star started.**
- St. John Chrysostom (*Homilies on Matthew*) notes the star behaved unlike any natural star, moving and stopping abnormally. His conclusion: a divine, supernatural sign, not an ordinary astrological event.

**The lesson of the Magi:** God reaches every nation, even through signs they understand, to bring them to His Son. Once they found Jesus, they worshipped Him. They did not go home to update their star charts. The star served its Maker; that is not astrology, that is the Author using His own creation for His own purposes.

## The Clear Orthodox Position

Astrology is incompatible with Christian faith. Consistent witness of Scripture, the Fathers, and the Councils. Not a grey area.

"Christian astrology" as a genuine category does not exist. What exists: people mixing Christian identity with astrological practice, which the Church calls away from.

This is not condemnation for past curiosity. The Church is a hospital. The question for a Christian moving forward: where do you seek guidance? The stars, or the God who made them?

The Church offers: the Jesus Prayer, Scripture, the Sacraments, your spiritual father. These connect you to the actual Source.

---

## God the Author Lens

God is so sovereign over the stars that He used one, supernaturally, to bring Gentile seekers to His Son. The stars served their Maker. That is not astrology. That is the Author using His own props to write His story.

**Key word:** *magoi* (Matthew 2:1, Greek) = learned men from the East who studied heavens and interpreted signs. Same root as "magic." The Magi's arrival at Christ is not a vindication of their art but its supersession by its Object.

## Reflection

Next time horoscopes come up in conversation or online: not condemnation for the curious. But a clear path. God alone knows your story. The stars He made are beautiful. He is not in them in the way astrology claims.
