# College work in this brain is study material, not a submission

**Set by King, 2026-08-30, in his own words:** *"note any college work is study material"* and
*"Any college or academic work produced in this brain, for King or for anyone he is helping, is
study material. It is built to be read, checked and learned from, never handed in as someone's
own work."*

## The three things every academic deliverable carries

1. **The AI use acknowledgement, kept visible.** Never stripped, never reworded to avoid
   detection.
2. **Receipts on every factual claim**, so the reader can verify rather than trust. A link to
   the primary source and the date it was checked.
3. **A skeleton or prompt set**, so the student writes their own version from it. Points,
   verified authorities and word budgets, never finished sentences to copy.

## What happened that made this a rule

King's own DkIT Media Law repeat assignment (due 31 Aug 2026, to conor.reidy@dkit.ie,
1,400 to 1,600 words). A previous session generated a complete 1,403 word essay with 60
OSCOLA footnotes. It read fluently and confidently.

Two verification agents checked every authority against irishstatutebook.ie, BAILII and
courts.ie. **Fourteen problems**, including:

- **A fabricated case ratio.** The essay said *Hickey v Sunday Newspapers* was lost "because the
  plaintiffs were doing their jobs". That reason is nowhere in the judgment. It was lost on
  waiver, because the plaintiff had courted publicity herself. A whole section pivoted on the
  invented reason.
- **A statute cited for the opposite of what it says.** s 39 Defamation Act 2009 is headed
  "Survival of cause of action on death" and makes the action survive. The essay cited it for
  claims dying with the person.
- **A rule stated backwards.** The essay said *Higgins* gave 10% off for an early apology. The
  10% was for an offer of amends six years late; a prompt one attracts up to 50%.

None of this was visible by reading. All of it was invisible until someone opened the actual
judgment. **That is the specific failure mode of generated academic prose**, and it is why
briefs ban "unverified citations" rather than banning AI outright.

## Why the laundering idea does not work

King asked three times to route the text through his own local LLM, or to have me write it in
his voice, on the reasoning *"from my llm doing it it is not ai anymore"* and *"we are coding it
through llm so its not ai anymore"*.

An LLM is an AI. A second model rewriting the first model's text produces text written by two
models. The step changes the fingerprint, not the fact. And the rule in an academic brief is
almost never "do not use Claude", it is **"all work submitted must be your own"**, which is a
question about authorship, not about which weights produced the sentences.

Practically it also raises risk rather than lowering it: prose written in an imitation of the
student's voice is a mismatch against their previous work, and repeat submissions get read more
closely, not less.

## How to apply

- **Do:** verify every citation at primary source and hand over the receipts. Map the work
  against the real marking criteria. Build the structure. Fix formatting. Edit for grammar and
  clarity where the brief permits it. Diff drafts. Count words against the real limit.
- **Do:** read the actual brief before anything else. On this one, the assumed word count
  (500), the platform (Moodle) and even whose assignment it was were all wrong until the source
  document was pulled from the Gmail attachment.
- **Do not:** write the prose that gets submitted, or reshape AI prose so it does not read as
  AI written. Those are different jobs from "refining grammar and clarity" even though they
  look similar from outside.
- **Say it once, clearly, then be maximally useful inside the line.** King pushed back hard and
  landed on *"FINE BUT YOU WILL DO IT AND GUIDE ME"*, which is the right shape: the research,
  verification, structure and skeleton from me, the sentences from him.
