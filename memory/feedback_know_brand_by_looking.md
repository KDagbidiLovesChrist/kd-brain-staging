# Know the brand by LOOKING · don't assume (King, 2026-06-28)

**The correction:** building The Buka's content, I set her brand palette to **red** off a guess. King:
*"This plan defeats you if you don't know what it is… you should know by looking at it."* I'd scraped her
Instagram **captions** (text) but never looked at the **post images**. When I downloaded and viewed her
actual flyers, her real brand was obvious: **forest green + gold + cream**, a mortar-and-pestle gold badge
logo, serif "THE BUKA" wordmark, "A Fresh Taste of Home." Red would have made every video off-brand.

**Why:** a brand lives in the VISUALS, not the caption text. Caption scraping ≠ knowing the brand. Assuming a
palette/look and building on it defeats the whole point, it produces off-brand work that has to be redone.

**How to apply:**
- For any client/brand that already has content, **pull their real post IMAGES and LOOK at them** (download +
  view, or `/watch`) before producing. Extract the true **palette (sample the colours), logo, fonts, tone**.
- Caption/text scraping is for menu/terms/keywords; the **brand identity comes from the images**.
- Lock it in a **Brand Sheet** from their REAL assets (not invented marks), then produce. This is the
  consistency lock the deep-storyboard standard wants.
- Same spirit as [[feedback-reference-driven-design]] (last 40% needs the real asset),
  [[feedback-confirm-spec-before-send]], and [[feedback-no-overselling-verify-before-send]], but specifically:
  **for brand colour/look, the source of truth is their actual content, so go look at it.**

**Buka specifics (corrected):** palette = forest green `#143D29` + gold `#C9A227` + cream `#F2E7CF`; mortar-pestle
badge logo + serif wordmark. Fixed `the-buka\workflow\BRAND_EDIT_SHEET.md` + `tools\build_buka_tutorial.py`.
